CYJ Labels · Buyer decision guide

Settle the content, container and revision before print

A chemical label order needs one released content version for each product and container layout. Start with the file approved by your responsible content owner, then connect it to the actual drum, jug or bottle. Keep artwork approval separate from the review of the applied label.

Decisions to settle before requesting production
DecisionWhat to defineInput or evidence
Content sourceApproved text, symbols, language and product identifierNamed content owner and released artwork reference
Container layoutUsable panel and a finished-size layout for each packageContainer photos, measurements and placement
Change and stockCurrent revision and disposition of older rollsBuyer-approved release decision and quantities by version

Checks to keep separate

If the content is still under review, prepare the container measurements first and hold the print release. A familiar pictogram or an old drum photograph is not the approved source file.

Prepare the buying brief

Prepare the approved artwork and revision, product list, container photos and dimensions, exposure details, quantity per layout and roll or sheet format.

Bring the released chemical artwork, not just a symbol list

A request for GHS chemical labels still needs the responsible owner to identify the required content and applicable market specification. Supply the exact approved artwork and revision for each product. If that content is unresolved, organize the container measurements while holding the print release.

Plan a separate finished-size proof for each drum, jug or bottle layout. Scaling one large label into a smaller area can change the reading hierarchy and usable code space. The content owner approves the message; the print proof checks how that supplied message is reproduced.

Chemical Label Applications

Chemical labels may be used for containers, drums, bottles, laboratory handling, product identification, batch information and safety communication.

  • Chemical container labels
  • Drum labels
  • Bottle labels
  • Lab handling labels
  • Batch and product identification
  • Warning and information labels

Information to Provide

Please send label size, shape, quantity, container material, surface condition, indoor or outdoor use, exposure conditions, exact printed content, artwork and any required customer specification.

Material and Adhesive Review

Chemical label construction should be discussed according to the container surface and expected exposure. Material, adhesive, lamination and finishing options must be confirmed for each project.

Separate Content Approval from Print Approval

Your responsible team should approve product identifiers, hazard text, symbols and language versions before the print proof is signed. The print proof then checks dimensions, legibility, layout and version. A printer cannot infer the correct hazards from a photo of the container.

Quote a Drum, Jug or Bottle Separately

A steel drum, plastic jug and small curved bottle may have different available label areas and surface conditions. Provide photographs and dimensions for each container, plus exposure to moisture, wiping, abrasion or incidental spills. Ask for a representative application sample when adhesion or readability is critical.
