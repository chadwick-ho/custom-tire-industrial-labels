Static Shear Data for Industrial Labels: Check the Load and Test Conditions

Published November 8, 2026

An equipment engineer receives two adhesive datasheets after noticing that a long label shifts slightly on a warm vertical panel. One supplier highlights a high peel figure. Another reports a long static shear time. The buyer needs to identify the kind of movement being investigated before comparing the evidence.

Ask what the shear test held constant

In a static shear test for a pressure-sensitive adhesive, a load acts along the bonded surface and the method defines the specimen, test conditions and endpoint. A peel result describes separation under a particular peel geometry. The peel comparison guide can help assess that second question when an edge is lifting.

A 3M transfer-tape datasheet shows why a buyer needs the condition with the number. It reports static shear measurements for the described tape at different temperatures. Those results belong to the stated material and method. A finished printed label has its own adhesive, face, substrate and service conditions.

Ask both suppliers for comparable evidence

Report fieldWhy it affects the decision
Test method and revisionDefines the setup and which endpoint is reported.
Bonded area and loadSets the load condition for the bonded strip.
Substrate and preparationIdentifies the tested interface and how it was prepared.
Dwell and temperatureEstablishes when the load was applied and under what environment.
EndpointDistinguishes measured failure from a test that ended while the specimen was still holding.

A result that says the specimen held until the test ended gives a lower bound for that test. A result that records failure after a particular time gives an observed endpoint. Ask whether the report distinguishes adhesive separation at an interface from failure within the adhesive. A purchasing comparison loses meaning when those results are treated as equivalent.

Decide whether a shear comparison fits the symptom

Capture paired photographs showing the label and a fixed reference on the panel if the label seems to have migrated. Record the surface finish, time, service temperature and label position. A curl at the exposed edge, a weak painted surface or a label contacted by moving equipment can need a different investigation. Ask the quality owner which observations would distinguish sliding from another mechanism.

For equipment labels, send the surface finish, proposed label construction, temperature range and evidence of the movement. Request material data with the conditions intact and agree what an applied trial on the actual panel needs to show before release.
