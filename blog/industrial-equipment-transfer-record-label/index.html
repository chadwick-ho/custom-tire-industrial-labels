Equipment Transfer Labels: Move a Copy to the Inspection Record

Published November 12, 2026

At a receiving bench, an operator records an equipment serial on a paper inspection record. A transferable label section could reduce manual entry, but the piece that leaves the machine must not take away the identifier maintenance needs later.

Map the two surfaces before comparing constructions

Decide which information remains visible on the installed label and which fields belong on the transferred piece. Ask the record owner whether a readable reference is sufficient or whether the transferred piece needs a machine-readable value too. Link both to the same approved source record and confirm that a repeat does not create a new asset.

Describe the application and transfer in order. Mark the part that lifts, the surface it is moved to and the person who confirms the result. The receiver may use paper, a job traveler or a clean container depending on the workflow. Ask for feasibility on both surfaces, including the period the piece remains attached before transfer.

HERMA’s documentation-label examples describe constructions where a section can be removed after the main label is applied and attached elsewhere. That shows a possible format. Have the supplier confirm whether an appropriate construction can be made for your equipment and record.

When to release the order

Hold when removal obscures required information or the transferred piece fails its record function. Use a separate matched set when pieces must go directly to two surfaces. If the station already scans an approved asset ID into its record, check whether the extra construction provides a practical benefit.
