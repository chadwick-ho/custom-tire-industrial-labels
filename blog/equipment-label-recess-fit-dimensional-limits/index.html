Equipment Labels in a Recess: Compare Dimensional Limits Before Approving Fit

Published November 20, 2026

A nominal equipment label fits a nominal recess drawing. Production samples can still be difficult to place if the smallest allowed recess and largest allowed label leave no practical room. Before accepting a tighter drawing or a cheaper cutting route, agree which size limits and installation allowance the purchase must meet.

Compare the limiting sizes without inventing a universal tolerance

Have the equipment owner provide the permitted recess dimensions and the clearance needed for installation and use. Ask the converter to identify achievable limits for the actual finished construction and cutting process. Record whether size is assessed on the liner, after removal or in another agreed state. Keep nominal width and height separate from the allowable upper and lower dimensions.

For a simple rectangular width screening, subtract the largest allowed label width from the smallest allowed recess width. In a fictional example, a recess of 50.0 ± 0.3 mm has a minimum width of 49.7 mm. A label of 49.0 ± 0.2 mm has a maximum width of 49.2 mm. The remaining total space is 0.5 mm. If perfectly centered it divides into 0.25 mm per side, but that arithmetic has not allowed for placement error, corner geometry or any owner's required clearance.

Repeat the relevant check for height and other constrained features. Let the drawing owner assess the actual geometry and any combined allowances; a simple width subtraction is not a full tolerance analysis. Review a representative finished sample on a suitable non-operational assembly under the owner's installation procedure. One sample fitting does not prove that all declared limits are compatible.

KEYENCE’s dimensional-tolerance explanation distinguishes nominal dimensions from allowable variation and inspection limits. The fictional dimensions above are an editorial arithmetic example, not a supplier capability or equipment requirement.

What to include in the approval record

Review pointEvidence to keep
RecessDrawing revision, allowable sizes and required clearance.
LabelFinished size limits, construction, process and measurement state.
Fit reviewLimiting-size comparison with placement and feature allowances.
ApprovalRepresentative sample, measurement record and named drawing owner.

Before approving the order

Hold dimensional release when the limits or installation allowance are missing. Ask whether a smaller label, altered layout or different supported process meets the requirement before ordering tighter tolerances. Do not shrink a code or warning merely to recover clearance without reviewing its separate readability requirements.
