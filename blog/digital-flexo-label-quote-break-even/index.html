Digital or Flexo Label Printing? Calculate From the Released SKU Quantities

Published November 7, 2026

A tire-label buyer has two quotations. One has a larger setup charge and a lower running price. The other has a smaller setup charge and a higher running price. The annual forecast looks large, but the immediate release contains several small SKU quantities. Choosing from the forecast total can produce the wrong comparison.

Calculate each confirmed offer against the work actually being released. Printing method can affect the cost structure, but the method name does not provide a universal break-even quantity. This guide does not establish which presses CYJ operates or promise a particular digital or flexographic route.

Request the same finished result first

BOBST's discussion of digital label production describes plate and setup considerations alongside alternatives to a simple short-run versus long-run split. It is a manufacturer's technology discussion, not a CYJ quotation or a market-wide price rule. Actual artwork, stock, finishing, converting and production arrangements still need confirmation.

Compare the same adhesive construction, print requirement, approved quantity by version, roll format, usable-count basis and delivery scope. If an offer changes the material or omits a required finish, resolve that difference before using a cost calculation to call it cheaper.

Use a transparent cost model

For a quotation with a genuinely fixed setup charge S and a constant price u per label within its stated range, the simplified total is S + u × Q. Q must use the quoted billing and quantity basis. Add separately quoted items once; do not add setup again if the supplier already included it in an all-in price.

These invented numbers illustrate arithmetic, not supplier prices. Under only these assumptions the lines meet at about 3,333 labels. A is not a definition of flexo and B is not a definition of digital. Quantity breaks, minimum charges, changing rates or different inclusions require a different calculation.

Keep the SKU mix in the calculation

Ten thousand labels for one stable design are not automatically the same setup request as ten designs of one thousand each. Ask which setup, plate, data-handling and finishing items are shared and which recur. Do not multiply every fee by the SKU count without confirmation, or assume every version shares one setup.

Use one row per released artwork or agreed production grouping. Record its quantity, confirmed fixed charges, applicable running rate and other included work. Sum the confirmed rows. Keep forecast-only quantities outside the calculation unless the buyer has expressly authorized their production and associated inventory.

Check the result against use, not only purchase price

A larger run may reduce a quoted unit price while creating unused old-version stock. A smaller release may increase setup frequency. Include only supportable inventory, disposal or handling costs; leave unknown costs visible instead of inventing savings. Physical suitability and approved data remain release conditions whichever offer has the lower calculated total.

For tire-label quotations, send a route-comparison brief with the released quantity per SKU, artwork revisions, exact construction, finish, roll requirements and both offers' inclusions. Ask which methods and cost arrangements are actually available. Hold a claimed crossover until the scope and rates are confirmed. Source checked November 7, 2026.
