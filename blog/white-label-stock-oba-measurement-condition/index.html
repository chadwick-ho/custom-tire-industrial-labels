White Label Stock Reports Need the UV Measurement Condition

Published November 22, 2026

A buyer approves an equipment-label proof on white stock, then receives a report whose white-point readings disagree with the converter’s report. Before asking for a new ink mix, have the colour reviewer check whether the two instruments used comparable measurement conditions. The stock’s appearance and the way it is measured can both matter.

Match the measurement condition before comparing readings

Identify the exact white material, printed or unprinted region, finish and reference sample. Ask whether the proposed stock contains optical brightening agents and what evidence supports that description. A bright-looking white is not proof of its formulation. Where the formulation is unknown, keep that uncertainty visible rather than diagnosing the disagreement by eye.

Esko’s colour-measurement guidance explains that UV in the measuring light can excite optical brighteners. It distinguishes M0 with unspecified UV content, M1 with controlled illumination appropriate to its stated application, and M2 with UV excluded. Record the actual condition used for both reference and trial. A condition for optically brightened paper does not automatically qualify fluorescent inks or every film construction.

Have the colour owner agree the instrument setup and available mode, sample backing, placement, measurement regions and calculation settings. Preserve the raw reported values and setup references. Do not convert an M0 reading into an M1 result by simply renaming the column or moving the tolerance. If a common supported condition cannot be produced, ask the reviewer for an agreed alternative evidence plan.

Repeat the comparison on the same controlled basis before changing stock or ink. Keep a visual comparison under the agreed viewing conditions where required. The numerical Delta E equation is a separate field in the record: matching the calculation name cannot correct inconsistent measurement illumination.

Esko Color Pilot’s measurement-condition documentation explains the UV and optical-brightener relationship and recommends consistent conditions for exchanged data. It is not a composition certificate for the quoted label.

What to include in the approval record

Review pointEvidence to keep
SampleExact stock, finish, region and controlled reference.
IlluminationActual M condition available and used on each instrument.
SetupBacking, aperture/geometry where relevant, locations and calculation settings.
DispositionComparable repeat evidence and named colour reviewer before an ink or stock change.

Before approving the order

Hold a rejection based on mismatched measurement conditions. If the agreed non-critical task only requires a controlled visual sample, keep that scope explicit. Do not promise a specific instrument mode or test report before the converter confirms it is available for the project.

Related reading includes the viewing-condition guide, the separate fluorescent-colour exposure guide, the numerical colour-acceptance guide.

For equipment machinery labels, send the white-stock reference, conflicting reports with setup details, intended finish, artwork and quantities for a colour-proof discussion.
