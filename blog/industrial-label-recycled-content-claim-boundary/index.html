Recycled-Content Label Claims: Name the Component and Percentage Basis

Published November 26, 2026

An industrial buyer receives a recycled-content statement for a proposed face material. The quotation covers a printed pressure-sensitive label on a release liner. Before adding a percentage to the artwork or comparing offers, identify the exact component and assessment basis the statement covers.

Define the content claim before comparing percentages

List the offered face, print and finish, adhesive and liner by their actual references. Ask which item the supplier’s recycled-content evidence describes. A statement about a face film does not automatically establish the same percentage for the complete printed label, the supplied roll or the equipment carrying it.

Retain the exact proposed wording, any pre-consumer or post-consumer distinction, the percentage basis and the responsible issuer. Ask how the evidence connects to the offered grade and order. Do not infer post-consumer content from a generic recycled-material description or treat a certification logo as a substitute for its relevant scope.

The US FTC Green Guides address substantiation and qualification of recycled-content claims, including distinguishing the scope of partially recycled items. They provide a useful claim-review example, not a certification of a label construction or a universal rule for every market. Have the buyer’s claims reviewer determine the applicable current requirements and approved wording.

When the supplier gives only a component percentage, keep it as a component statement unless the qualified reviewer has the complete evidence for a broader claim. Do not average percentages across layers without the relevant quantities, boundaries and approved calculation basis. A missing component is unknown, not automatically zero or fully recycled.

Compare offers using a small content register rather than a single “green” column. Record material reference, claim subject, content type, stated basis, supporting document and unresolved gaps. Keep the functional label review separate: content evidence does not establish adhesion, print durability, code readability or compatibility with the intended equipment.

FTC Green Guides, section 260.13 discuss recycled-content claims and their qualification. They are guidance, not product approval. Current market-specific wording and evidence acceptance remain with the responsible claims reviewer.

What to include in the approval record

Review pointEvidence to keep
SubjectExact component or complete item described, with offered construction references.
BasisStated percentage, denominator and any pre-/post-consumer distinction.
EvidenceIssuer, relevant grade/order scope, date and documented limitations.
ArtworkExact approved wording and claim owner, separate from ordinary print sign-off.

Before approving the order

Hold unsupported environmental wording while the material inquiry continues. Neutral artwork can remain an option while the supplier and claims owner resolve what evidence is available. This page does not establish that CYJ supplies a particular recycled-content grade.

Recycled content and recyclability answer different questions. A supported incoming-content statement does not establish a collection route, compostability, lower carbon footprint or a claim about the attached equipment.

Related reading includes the end-of-use claim review, the sourcing-mark approval guide, the label construction brief.

For materials adhesives, send the proposed construction, exact content wording, supplier documents and assessment basis with the functional requirements and quantities.
