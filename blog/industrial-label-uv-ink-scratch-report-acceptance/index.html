Scratch-Resistant Equipment Label Claims Need the Actual Contact and Test Scope

Published November 18, 2026

A finished equipment label has a narrow line through part of its legend after a defined handling step. The supplier offers a scratch-resistant ink claim and an adhesion report. Ask whether the evidence describes the damaging contact the buyer expects and the actual printed construction being proposed.

Compare the damage mode before comparing the results

Keep the affected label and document the contact path, process stage and information that became unreadable. A narrow scratch, broad rubbing and ink removed by a tape procedure can require different evidence. Ask the supplier to identify the method and complete specimen behind its scratch claim, including ink, substrate and any protective layer.

FINAT separately lists a UV ink-film scratch method and an ink-rub method. A report on exposed UV ink should retain that scope when a laminate is proposed. Have the qualified test owner decide whether the existing report supports the order or whether another applicable assessment is needed. Under an approved plan, evaluate a representative finished label after the real contact step. Inspect the legend and code independently from whether the label stays attached.

FINAT’s technical handbook overview lists FTM 29 for UV ink-film scratch resistance and FTM 27 for UV ink rubbing. It provides method names rather than universal performance limits.

What to include in the approval record

Review pointEvidence to keep
DamageRetained sample, contact shape and path, process stage and affected fields.
ReportApplicable method and tested ink, substrate and finish references.
ComparisonMatching specimen or a documented difference from the ordered construction.
ReleaseApproved finished-label observations and required code or text checks.

Before approving the order

Hold a scratch-resistance claim if its only support is a tape-adhesion result or a different exposed surface. Where the real contact cannot be reproduced, preserve the limitation and ask the process owner what evidence is sufficient for a limited release.

Related reading includes the rubbing and transfer guide, the tape-adhesion evidence guide, the protective finish comparison.

For equipment machinery labels, send the affected and proposed label references, contact photos, supplier report and the intended service conditions.
