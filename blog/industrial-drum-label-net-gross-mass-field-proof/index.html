Industrial Drum Labels: Keep Net Mass Separate From Gross Weight and Capacity

Published November 21, 2026

A drum-label file contains a scale reading, an empty-container value and a nominal capacity. The layout has one field called WEIGHT. Before the converter populates it, have the product and shipping owners decide what that field describes. A packed-unit measurement and a container's nominal volume are not interchangeable with the mass of its contents.

Name the object behind each quantity

Define the approved field headings and source records. Net mass concerns the contents under the agreed definition, while a gross figure includes the packaging covered by that measurement. Identify which drum, closure or other packaging the approved tare includes. Nominal container capacity is a volume-related description and does not by itself provide a contents mass.

In a fictional mass-only example, an approved gross value of 212 kg and an approved applicable tare of 12 kg give 200 kg by subtraction. This is arithmetic, not an actual weighing result or a production-fill instruction. The source owner must determine whether those measurements and their basis are applicable to the same filled unit. Do not copy a tare from a different drum design or infer mass from litres without an authorized conversion basis.

If the receiving program requests GS1 data, have its owner specify the appropriate field, units and precision for the intended quantity. The printed field and encoded attribute should refer to the same approved object and value. Keep a gross logistics-unit field separate from a net trade-item field where both are needed. Check the actual parsed receiving result as well as the readable label.

GS1’s General Specifications attribute reference distinguishes net-weight and gross-weight attributes and their units. It identifies field meanings; it does not choose the product's declared amount, weighing procedure or applicable legal quantity requirement.

What to include in the approval record

Review pointEvidence to keep
QuantityOwner-approved meaning, object, heading and unit.
SourceApplicable gross/tare or released net record for the actual unit.
CapacitySeparate approved volume description if the layout requires one.
Output witnessPrinted value, encoded field and observed receiving interpretation.

Before approving the order

Hold if a bare scale number is supplied without its measurement basis or a volume is being relabeled as kilograms. Return content declarations and any compliance question to the responsible product owner. The printer should reproduce approved information, not determine fill quantities or chemical handling instructions.

Related reading includes the value-and-unit guide, the numeric import guide, the chemical drum brief.

For chemical labels, send the approved quantity definitions, harmless source records, required barcode fields, drum layout and label quantities.
