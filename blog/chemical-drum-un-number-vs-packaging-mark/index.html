Chemical Drum Artwork: Separate the Contents UN Number From the Packaging Mark

Published November 25, 2026

A chemical-label buyer sends a drum photograph and asks the designer to copy “the UN code.” The photograph contains information about the contents and a separate packaging marking. Identify the requested item before turning either reference into production artwork.

Name the information and its owner

A hazardous-material identification number and a performance-packaging marking answer different questions. The first identifies the material under the applicable transport classification. The second carries information about the packaging design and its specified marking basis. Similar letters do not make them interchangeable.

Ask the qualified contents or transport reviewer to provide the approved identification and artwork for the actual material and shipment. Ask the packaging owner or supplier to identify the drum’s existing marking and the records that support it. A photograph can locate the information, but it cannot authorize a designer to choose a classification or claim packaging performance.

Keep those references separate in the artwork brief. Record whether the label order is reproducing owner-approved contents information, whether it must leave an existing packaging marking visible, and whether any requested packaging-mark reproduction has a separately authorized scope. Do not build a packaging performance code from fragments copied from another drum.

PHMSA’s 2018 How to Use the HMR glossary identifies the UN/NA identification-number concept. It is terminology context, not a complete current classification or shipment instruction. The actual applicability and marking requirements belong with the qualified transport and packaging owners.

Review the finished label position on the actual drum. Mark other existing information in the placement photograph and ask the responsible owner to approve the arrangement. A larger replacement sticker should not silently turn a separate packaging reference into a claim about the new contents or conceal information the owner requires to remain accessible.

PHMSA’s Unpacking Packaging Codes guide explains the performance-packaging marking and its components. It does not certify a particular drum or establish its compatibility with a proposed chemical.

What to include in the approval record

Review pointEvidence to keep
Contents recordQualified owner’s approved material identification and transport artwork reference.
Packaging recordActual drum identity, existing marking and responsible packaging supplier or owner.
Artwork scopePrecisely what is reproduced, what remains separate and the authority for any claimed marking.
Installed witnessMarked drum photograph, final label footprint and owner-approved arrangement.

Before approving the order

Hold artwork when the request contains only an unexplained UN reference or a copied packaging code. Return the ambiguity to its respective owner before quoting an authorized print scope. A label proof cannot establish that a drum is suitable for a chemical shipment.

For a simple internal product or inventory sticker, retain its limited purpose rather than adding transport or packaging claims from a reference photograph. This guide does not select packaging, classify dangerous goods or authorize a certification marking.

For chemical labels, send the owner-approved contents artwork, a separate packaging-reference photo, the marked placement area, label construction and quantities.
