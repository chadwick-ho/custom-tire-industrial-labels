Rotary Abrasion Reports for Labels Need the Endpoint as Well as the Cycle Count

Published November 23, 2026

A buyer compares two protective finishes for an equipment label. One report lists a mass loss after a stated abrasion exposure; another lists cycles until a defined change. The larger cycle number does not by itself identify the better label. First establish what each result measures and whether the compared specimens and test conditions are equivalent.

Make the contact and endpoint comparable

Request the exact test method and revision, abrading wheel or material, applied load, cycle count, specimen preparation and relevant conditioning. Identify the exposed surface, including any laminate or varnish. A report on an unprinted protective film may help a material discussion without proving that the printed assembly keeps its information readable.

Taber Industries describes rotary abrasion with two wheels contacting a rotating flat specimen. It separately lists evaluation by mass loss, appearance or a specific endpoint, among other methods. A fixed number of cycles followed by weighing is not the same claim as continuing until a particular feature is lost. Ask the test owner to state the actual endpoint and reporting units before ranking reports.

For the purchase, mark the critical legend and code area on the specimen drawing. Have the reviewer define whether those fields are inspected after a specified exposure, at a particular endpoint, or under a separate representative handling trial. Do not assume the wear track passed over the information just because the specimen had some printing elsewhere.

Keep surface wear, readable text, decoded payload and attachment as distinct observations. A low material mass loss can coexist with damage to a small critical field. A cosmetic mark outside the required information may have a different disposition under the agreed specification. The responsible quality owner, rather than a generic cycle-count rule, determines acceptance.

Taber Industries’ rotary-abraser description and evaluation guidance explains the apparatus and distinct result types. It does not establish that CYJ performs this test or that a reported cycle count predicts field life.

What to include in the approval record

Review pointEvidence to keep
SpecimenFinished exposed construction, mounting and critical-field locations.
ProcedureMethod/revision, wheel, load, preparation and conditioning.
ResultFixed exposure or defined endpoint, units and observations actually retained.
ReleaseOwner-approved information, code and attachment criteria plus any use trial.

Before approving the order

Hold a like-for-like comparison if setup or endpoint information is absent. Where the real problem is one handling contact, a representative contact witness may be more useful than purchasing an unrelated laboratory number. Retain any limits when using a report to screen alternatives.

For equipment machinery labels, send both reports, proposed constructions, critical artwork fields, real contact path, dimensions and quantities for a finish-evidence review.
