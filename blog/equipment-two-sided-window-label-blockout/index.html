Two-Sided Equipment Window Labels: Check Each Message and the Layer Between

Published November 12, 2026

A transparent enclosure panel needs one message visible to an operator outside and another visible to a service technician on the inside. The printed proof looks fine when each design is viewed alone. The installed sample needs to prove that both messages remain legible together.

Approve the two viewing faces in an installed drawing

On a section drawing, mark the reader on each side, the actual mounting surface and the intended reading orientation. Provide separate controlled artwork for both messages. Where a supplier proposes reverse printing, have prepress confirm which design is reversed for the assembly. Compare the specimen from each reader’s position after it is mounted.

Review both messages with the actual panel tint, background and lighting. Identify the area where an opaque separator is allowed and where visibility through the panel must remain. If a barcode is needed, include the actual reader and the intended label face. Confirm any required equipment instructions separately with the technical owner.

Drytac’s opaque-film product information describes a two-sided window graphic made by pairing an opaque layer with a reverse-printed clear film. Use it as a construction example and ask the converter to confirm options for your actual equipment size and environment.

Review pointEvidence to record before ordering
Outside faceReleased artwork, orientation and reading position through or against the installed panel.
Inside faceSecond released design and its viewing and service conditions.
SeparationPermitted opaque zone, alignment, opposing artwork show-through and overall applied thickness.

When to release the order

Hold if the artwork is readable only when laid flat, one message bleeds through the other or the opacity obscures a required sightline. Ask the converter to explain the finished layer order and obtain the equipment owner’s approval of both views. If only one side is read, a single-message window label is simpler to review.
