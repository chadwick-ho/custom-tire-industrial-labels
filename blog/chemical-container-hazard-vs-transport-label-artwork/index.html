Chemical Container Labels: Keep Product Hazard and Transport Artwork in Separate Review

Published November 6, 2026

A drum-label enquiry includes a chemical product panel and a second sheet of transport graphics. Both contain diamond-shaped elements. The artwork team is asked to combine them, remove apparent duplicates and make the whole design smaller. That is a content-approval decision before it is a printing decision.

Keep the product hazard information and the transport marking or label requirements identifiable during the review. Their appearance does not determine their purpose, applicability or placement. A printer should not delete or replace one because another graphic looks similar.

Assign the two content questions

PHMSA's joint-agency overview explains that US DOT hazard communication and OSHA hazard-communication labels can appear on the same package. The overview was issued in 2018. It illustrates why two review scopes may coexist; it is not a complete current labelling decision for a particular chemical shipment.

Ask the buyer's chemical-compliance owner to approve product hazard content and its intended use. Ask the qualified transport owner to establish the applicable shipment requirements for the actual substance, package, route and mode. Have those owners resolve coexistence, layout and any conflicting instructions under the current rules. Do not infer a transport classification from a pictogram or this article.

Map each approved item to the package it belongs on

Artwork itemIdentify before layoutApproval needed
Product hazard panelProduct, content version, language and containerResponsible chemical-content reviewer
Transport marking or labelExact required artwork and applicable package levelQualified transport reviewer
Outer package or overpack informationActual packing arrangement and required visibilityOwner of the shipping configuration
Traceability and variable fieldsApproved product, batch and shipment data relationshipsRelevant data owner

The table proposes a coordination record, not a regulatory checklist. Record not applicable only when the responsible reviewer has determined that result. Unknown is a useful status when the packing arrangement or transport decision is still unsettled.

Use a package photograph alongside the flat proof

Show the actual drum, container, carton or proposed outer assembly, including closures, handles, seams, straps and other existing information. Identify what remains visible in the final shipping state. A flat composite PDF cannot establish that a required item is readable after the package is wrapped or assembled.

Do not resize required graphics or delete approved wording to create room for branding without the content owners' agreement. If the available space conflicts with the approved requirements, return the package and layout question for review before cutting tools or bulk stock are ordered.

Approve printing only after the content conflict is resolved

Release one complete artwork revision with a clear record of the approved items and package levels. Preserve the supplied source graphics rather than redrawing a transport symbol from a low-resolution photograph. Keep artwork reproduction, classification and shipment authorization as separate responsibilities.

Material selection remains another task. Specify container surface, application conditions, handling and exposure. Where a buyer requires marine-use durability evidence, review the exact standard and construction through the BS 5609 document guide. A durability report cannot decide which hazard or transport content belongs on a shipment.

For custom chemical-container labels, send a package-and-artwork brief with approved content files, package photos, owner-approved placement requirements, language versions, surface, dimensions and quantities. Keep unapproved transport decisions out of the production release. Source checked November 6, 2026; current applicability and content must be confirmed by the responsible reviewers.
