Equipment Labels Near a Paint Process Need a PWIS Contact-Scope Review

Published November 23, 2026

A component supplier needs an identification label on a valve that will enter a customer’s paint-sensitive production area. Purchasing asks for silicone-free labels. Before treating that phrase as sufficient evidence, obtain the customer’s actual paint-wetting impairment requirement and describe how the label could contact the process. A materials description and an application-specific contamination assessment are different deliverables.

Start with the actual contact route and required assessment

Have the paint-process owner identify the installation position, whether the label face or another exposed surface can contact paint, and any relevant indirect route. Include the stage when the liner is removed and how the labeled part is handled and packed. Do not presume that a label on the outside of a component has no connection to the process merely because it is not intentionally painted.

Ask for the applicable customer specification, revision, product group, test class and acceptance route. PWIS, also called LABS, concerns substances that can impair paint wetting. A claim about silicone alone does not define every substance or transfer route covered by that requirement. The qualified process owner should decide what evidence is necessary and which supplied or installed state must be represented.

VDMA’s practical guide specifically discusses adhesive labels and distinguishes material on the adhesive interface from possible contamination of the exposed label surface. Its examples require assessment of the actual contact situation. Do not use a generally described class in a guide as approval for a particular order, or infer that every liner and label construction behaves identically.

Give the supplier the complete construction and handling brief. Request evidence tied to the offered film, adhesive, print, finish and supply format, with any limitations made explicit. If a sample assessment is required, arrange it with the responsible test owner before release. CYJ’s general equipment-label offering does not establish a PWIS-qualified production process or a contamination-test capability.

VDMA’s practical guide to paint-wetting impairment substances, section 5.2 provides an adhesive-label example. Use the requirement owner’s applicable specification and revision for the actual assessment.

What to include in the approval record

Review pointEvidence to keep
ApplicationLabel position, process stage and direct or indirect contact routes.
RequirementCustomer specification, revision, product group, class and approver.
SpecimenExact construction and relevant supplied/installed condition.
EvidenceIdentified assessment, limitations and accepted handling or alternative marking route.

Before approving the order

Hold a PWIS-conformity statement when only a generic silicone-free description is available. Where an adhesive label cannot be justified, ask the engineering owner about an alternative marking or protected location. A normal warehouse ID outside the paint-sensitive scope should retain its own relevant label requirements.

Related reading includes the paint-mask process guide, the release-liner performance guide, the materials-document scope guide.

For equipment machinery labels, send the customer contamination requirement, part and label-location photos, handling route, proposed construction, dimensions and quantities for an equipment-label review.
