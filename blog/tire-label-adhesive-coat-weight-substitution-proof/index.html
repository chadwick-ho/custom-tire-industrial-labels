Tire Label Adhesive Coat Weight: What a Matched Number Can Establish

Published November 17, 2026

A supplier offers a new tire label construction with the same adhesive coat weight as the approved stock. The figure can help check the proposed construction against a purchasing specification. It cannot establish how the finished label will perform on the buyer’s cured rubber, particularly when other parts of the construction change.

Use the number for the property it measures

Ask the supplier to identify whether the value describes dry adhesive mass per unit area, which construction and lot it represents, and which method was used. Confirm that the number is for the bonding adhesive and is distinguished from liner silicone coating or a facestock basis weight. If coat weight is a purchasing requirement, agree the stated target, evidence and acceptance responsibility with the supplier. FINAT treats adhesive coat weight and peel adhesion as separate test methods.

Compare the current and proposed constructions using the same tread location, sample geometry, application conditions and expected handling. Record the adhesive and the complete face and liner references. Have the buyer review initial placement, edge behavior and the condition after its normal handling interval. Evaluate roll dispensing if that function matters. The resulting witness connects the purchase to what the plant will actually apply.

The FINAT technical handbook overview lists FTM 12 for adhesive coat weight separately from peel, shear, and liner-release tests. Match the method and construction when comparing reports.

What to include in the approval record

Review pointEvidence to keep
Measured layerAdhesive coat versus facestock, liner or liner coating.
Comparison basisMethod, units, construction, lot and supplier’s declared specification.
Tread sampleRepresentative cured rubber and the buyer’s intended application and handling.
DispositionApproval scope, evidence and owner of any requalification.

Before approving the order

Hold a substitution when the only equivalence evidence is the coat-weight number. A coating report can be used as an agreed supplier control once the actual construction and its intended use have been qualified.
