A UL 94 Rating on a Label Film: Check the Material and Thickness

Published November 15, 2026

An electrical equipment buyer asks for a flame-rated label. The quotation attaches a UL 94 statement for a familiar polymer name. Before accepting it, have the equipment compliance owner identify the requirement and check which exact material and thickness the evidence describes.

Resolve the required assessment before selecting a grade

Obtain the requirement from the responsible equipment owner, including the intended use and evidence format. Request the actual material grade, manufacturer, documented thickness and the relevant rating record. Keep those details linked to the proposed label film. A polymer family name leaves open which formulation and specimen the rating concerns.

List the other layers in the supplied construction and ask the reviewer whether the cited evidence addresses them or whether another assessment is required. A film result does not describe every possible adhesive, ink or protective layer added to it. Record any conditions and limits in the purchase approval. Where durable marking recognition is also requested, obtain the relevant system documentation through that separate review.

UL Solutions’ explanation of UL 94 ratings and limitations describes small-scale testing of plastic materials and the limits of applying those results. The equipment reviewer must assess the actual end use.

Review pointEvidence to keep
RequirementOwner-approved flammability requirement for the intended equipment use.
MaterialManufacturer, grade, thickness and identifiable supporting record.

Before approving the order

Hold if the offered record cannot be tied to the quoted material or the reviewer’s required assessment. A shop-floor flame demonstration cannot replace the required evidence. Keep authorized certification wording under the compliance owner’s control.

For electronics electrical labels, send the equipment owner’s required assessment, proposed construction and the available material record.
