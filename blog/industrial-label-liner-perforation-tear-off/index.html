Perforated Label Liners: Agree What Tears and What Stays Intact

Published November 5, 2026

The buyer wants operators to tear off one labelled section at a time for an equipment kit. The drawing shows a dashed line between labels. It does not say which layers the line passes through or what must remain intact during printing.

Specify the purpose, location and affected layers of a proposed perforation before asking for production. A dashed line in the artwork is not enough. Have the supplier assess the construction and the equipment owner confirm that the finished media can travel through the intended process.

Name the separation task

Separating a section of liner carrying an intact label is different from tearing through a label face. Opening part of the backing to expose an adhesive strip is another task again. Describe the action the operator needs to perform rather than using perforated as a complete specification.

HERMA's roll-label page identifies punching and perforation as application-specific options within its own service. This establishes that such features are defined converting choices. It does not confirm their availability, geometry or machine suitability for a CYJ order.

Put the feature on a controlled drawing

Show the feed direction, label outline, spacing and proposed separating line. State whether the required result is a detached section, a partially opened liner or another defined presentation. The converter should assess the detailed perforation design; this article supplies no cut-to-tie ratio or universal tear-force requirement.

Keep required print and encoded data away from any affected area according to the approved layout. A drawing showing an intact barcode before separation does not prove it remains intact afterward. Include the actual handling action in the sample review.

Check printing before easy tearing

A feature that is convenient at the bench may be unsuitable for the web path of a printer or applicator. Give the equipment owner the complete proposed media specification and ask which evaluation is required. A manual tear demonstration alone cannot approve feeding, sensing or dispensing.

Where the requirement is purely manual, agree how sections are supplied, counted and kept identifiable after separation. If multiple label versions are involved, a detached section still needs a reliable relationship to the intended equipment item or kit.

Use a representative sample record

  1. Identify the sample, drawing revision, construction and intended print method.
  2. Record which layers are meant to separate and the intended location.
  3. Have the authorized equipment owner assess processing where equipment is involved.
  4. Perform the agreed manual separation and inspect the resulting label, data and backing.
  5. Record approval scope, exceptions and any redesign needed before production.

Do not describe an unexplained tear as an intentional perforation merely because it appears between labels. Compare the received stock with the approved drawing. The liner-condition receiving guide covers preserving evidence when a supplied condition is in doubt.

Allow a simpler format to win

If the operator only needs a few labels at a time, a suitable sheet or another confirmed pack arrangement may answer the task with fewer special features. If machine use requires an intact web, discuss whether the desired handoff can happen later in the process. Compare the actual workflow, sample requirements and quoted cost rather than assuming perforation is always an improvement.

Send a separation-format enquiry with label dimensions, quantities by version, a marked drawing, equipment details and a description of what the operator must tear or peel. Ask for feasibility and a representative sample before treating the feature as approved.
