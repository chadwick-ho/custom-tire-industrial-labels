Tire Label Proofs: Keep Maximum Pressure and Vehicle Recommendations Separate

Published November 12, 2026

A distributor’s artwork file has a box labeled “pressure” beside the tire size. The value was copied from a vehicle example, but the same tire SKU is sold for different vehicles. The artwork owner needs to define or remove that field before releasing the optional label.

Identify the source and the job of each value

Separate a tire-specific marking from the vehicle manufacturer’s recommendation. Ask the responsible product owner to identify the exact tire model, variant, data source and approved wording. If the brief asks for vehicle-specific information, involve the vehicle documentation owner and record which vehicle and configuration the statement applies to. A line of artwork does not establish a new service instruction.

Keep numeric values paired with their approved meaning, units and source revision. Review the field in each SKU proof rather than copying it across variants. Do not present a generic pressure instruction alongside optional product identification. The content owner can omit an optional field when there is no verified purpose or source.

Michelin’s tire pressure guidance distinguishes sidewall maximum pressure from the recommended vehicle pressure on the vehicle placard. Use the relevant tire and vehicle manufacturer records for your artwork review.

Review pointEvidence to record before ordering
Tire fieldExact tire variant, manufacturer data revision and approved wording.
Vehicle fieldApplicable vehicle and configuration, source owner and purpose if the field is required.
ProofUnambiguous wording and units, independent SKU comparison and technical sign-off.

When to release the order

Hold if a number appears without a source or could be read as a vehicle instruction. Keep required molded markings and vehicle placards separate from the optional sticker. This proof check is for purchasing and artwork. Vehicle use follows the vehicle manufacturer’s instructions.
