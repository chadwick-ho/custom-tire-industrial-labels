A Halogen-Free Label Request Needs a Definition and a Complete Print Construction

Published November 22, 2026

An electronics OEM asks for halogen-free serial labels. A quote includes a compliant-sounding film description and a RoHS statement, but the marking will be thermal-transfer printed. Before accepting the offer, obtain the customer’s actual halogen criterion and identify which supplied layers and transferred print the evidence covers. The phrase alone does not define a complete purchasing specification.

Approve the definition before the material name

Ask the OEM’s requirement owner for the referenced specification, revision, substances, limits, assessment basis and required evidence. Confirm whether the request concerns a named industry definition or a broader customer prohibition. Do not expand a chlorine-and-bromine criterion into a claim that every halogen is absent, and do not infer a halogen assessment from an unrelated restriction statement.

Build the evidence map around the actual supplied label: face, adhesive, any protective or printed layers and the print system. In thermal transfer, identify the ribbon whose image-forming material remains on the label. A statement for the blank face material cannot silently cover a separately chosen print consumable. The discarded carrier and finished transferred image are different objects; have the qualified reviewer define the assessment boundary.

Brady’s R6000 Halogen Free ribbon data sheet gives a named ribbon its own definition and supporting references. It also distinguishes that claim from other regulatory information. This is evidence that print consumables need an identified basis, not proof that CYJ uses the product or that the finished label offered for your order meets the same requirement.

Request a declaration or report tied to the actual item references and revisions, with gaps listed explicitly. The customer’s reviewer decides whether the submitted evidence is sufficient. Keep functional approval separate: a material-composition statement does not establish adhesion, readable print, flame behavior or service life on the intended equipment.

Brady’s R6000 Halogen Free technical data sheet, dated March 18, 2022 is a product-specific example of defined terminology. Its claims and approvals do not transfer to another ribbon, stock or converter.

What to include in the approval record

Review pointEvidence to keep
RequirementCustomer specification, revision, substances, limits and assessment boundary.
ConstructionNamed face, adhesive, protective layers and transferred printing system.
EvidenceItem-specific declaration/report references, dates and uncovered components.
ReleaseCustomer’s materials reviewer plus separate functional label acceptance.

Before approving the order

Hold the halogen claim if the definition is missing or evidence only covers a different stock. Where no such requirement exists, choose materials by the actual application rather than adding an unsupported badge. Confirm what documentation is available before promising the customer a particular statement.
