RFID Tire Logistics Labels Need a Separate Record for Voided Output

Published November 18, 2026

A tire warehouse’s existing RFID logistics printer can eject a rejected smart label and try the same record on another piece. The station may therefore consume more physical labels than the number of accepted business identities. Settle how the visible fallback labels and the rejected pieces are accounted for before approving the supply and job.

Witness the configured exception path

Have the RFID and print-system owners explain the installed printer’s retry and stop behavior. Record whether it retries the same data, advances to another record, stops for an operator or reports an exception elsewhere. Use an authorized trial that keeps all specimens away from live inventory. Let the equipment owner select a suitable way to demonstrate a failure and its recovery.

For each trial record, reconcile the physically rejected pieces, the accepted encoded piece and its readable or optical identity. Keep rejected output segregated under the warehouse procedure. A VOID mark is useful only when staff recognize it and the system’s disposition remains unambiguous. Confirm the outcome when the configured attempt limit is reached. The released record should show accepted units separately from consumed stock and printing exceptions.

Zebra’s RFID Programming Guide 3 describes model-dependent error handling and retries under the RFID setup commands. Ask the installed-system owner to confirm the current configuration.

What to include in the approval record

Review pointEvidence to keep
ConfigurationPrinter, firmware, qualified smart stock and approved exception behavior.
Trial recordReleased identifier and the expected result of a failed attempt.
Physical accountingRejected pieces, accepted retry and any unresolved output.
ReleaseOptical-to-RFID association, accepted quantity and responsible approver.

Before approving the order

Hold if a failed piece can enter usable stock or an identifier is accepted on two physical units contrary to the approved plan. Printed-only tire labels can use an optical exception workflow. This guide concerns an existing RFID system with its own specialist supply owner.

Related reading includes the inlay-position qualification guide, the identifier mapping guide, the interrupted serial-job guide.

For tire barcode labels, send the approved visible-label fields, exception workflow and accepted-unit requirement, with the smart-stock drawing if available.
