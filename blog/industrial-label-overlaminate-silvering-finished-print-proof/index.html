Industrial Label Overlaminates: Check Haze on the Finished Printed Sample

Published November 17, 2026

A protective film is added over a dark equipment label. The finished sample shows small bright specks across an area that looked uniform before the film was applied. Purchasing needs to decide whether that effect changes the appearance or reading task, and what printed sample the converter should submit as proof.

Compare the same printed region before and after lamination

Retain a print from before lamination and an adjacent witness made with the approved film and process. Mark whether the haze concentrates on heavy ink, follows the web or appears in otherwise unprinted areas. View both versions under the same agreed conditions. On the finished label, inspect readable text and test any code using the intended setup. Keep any applicable formal verification separate from an appearance judgment.

Avery Dennison’s graphics bulletin describes how air can be trapped in the surface texture of some UV ink prints under a laminate, creating a silvered appearance. Haze alone cannot identify the cause. Ask the converter to review the specific print, laminate and process and produce a matching finished proof for any proposed change. Where a defect appears only after mounting, keep installed-surface effects in the investigation.

Avery Dennison’s bulletin on overlaminating printed graphics explains silvering in specified film and ink applications. Its settings are product-specific and need confirmation before use elsewhere.

What to include in the approval record

Review pointEvidence to keep
PrintSame production print and marked dark and light areas for comparison.
FilmActual laminate and converted label revisions.
ObservationAppearance, light/angle, and when haze first appears.
AcceptanceBuyer-approved visual conditions, readable fields and any required code result.

Before approving the order

Hold a finish change when only the unlaminated print has been approved. If the appearance meets the agreed condition and every required field passes its checks, document that result on the actual finished sample.

Related reading includes the finish selection guide, the print process release guide, the delayed installed bubble guide.

For equipment machinery labels, send matched before-and-after samples, the print and laminate references, photographs and the intended reading conditions.
