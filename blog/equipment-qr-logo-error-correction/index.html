A Logo Inside an Equipment QR Code? Approve the Undecorated Code First

Published November 6, 2026

An equipment-label proof has a large QR symbol. Marketing wants to place the company logo in its centre, and someone says a high error-correction level makes the change safe. The maintenance team needs the label to work after normal handling, when the surface may no longer be pristine.

Approve the functional code before approving decoration. The buying decision is whether a proposed graphic modification is justified in this particular identification workflow, and what evidence would support it. There is no universal safe logo-area percentage for a custom equipment label.

Correction capability is not a blank design area

DENSO WAVE explains that QR error correction concerns recovery of codewords, and that higher levels can increase the symbol size. Its FAQ on inserted illustrations warns that deformation uses correction capability and can impair reading. These are reasons to assess the actual generated symbol, not to calculate an allowed overlay from the outside area of a square.

A logo is deliberate obstruction present before the label enters service. Do not count that obstruction as if it were reserve capacity still available for later damage. Avoid claiming a finished symbol tolerates a particular percentage of scratches from the correction setting alone.

Compare three layouts using one approved payload

Start with the exact approved destination or identifier. Generate an undecorated reference appropriately for that data and intended physical size. Then compare the proposed overlay and a third layout with the logo outside the symbol. Keep the label construction, finish and reading task consistent so the comparison answers the graphic question.

These are comparison options, not approved constructions. If the adjacent-logo layout crowds the clear area, enlarge or rearrange the label rather than assuming that moving the logo automatically resolves the problem. Preserve readable equipment identification as a fallback.

Evaluate the final output at its point of use

Have the workflow owner define representative devices, application software, placement, lighting and approach positions. Use finished-size printed samples on the intended equipment surface. Record decoding, the exact returned data and the application result separately. A camera displaying a preview is not proof that the technician reached the correct equipment record.

If service exposure or formal print-quality verification is part of the order, agree those methods and criteria before the trial. Keep the generated source, overlay revision, sample identity and observations together. Do not describe one successful phone scan as a durability or universal-reader guarantee.

When to hold the design

Hold an overlay proposal when the responsible reviewer has not accepted the method, the intended reader fails, the output returns the wrong record, or the required exposure evidence is missing. Keeping the symbol undecorated is a practical design option while the branding team uses another part of the label. A functional identification requirement should have a named approver, even when the artwork looks attractive.

For an industrial QR label discussion, send one QR artwork brief with the approved payload, unmodified source, proposed logo placement, finished dimensions, equipment photo, reader workflow and quantity.
