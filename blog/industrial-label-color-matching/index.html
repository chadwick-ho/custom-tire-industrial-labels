A Brand Color Is Not Only a Hex Code

A color seen in a browser, a design file and a finished label may not be experienced the same way. The face stock, ink system, finish, ambient light and viewing angle all shape the final result. A useful buyer brief identifies the approved reference and states which product version or existing sample is the comparison point.

A Composite Proof Review: The Blue Was Right Until It Met the Film

In a composite project, the marketing file contains a familiar deep blue. The first proof looks close on paper, then the label changes to a different film finish and the visual impression shifts. Neither team is being difficult; they are comparing different surfaces. The decision is to lock the color review to the intended material and finish rather than approve a color in isolation.

Give the Factory One Clear Reference

Do not send several screenshots and ask the factory to choose the closest version. Provide the selected color standard or approved physical sample where available, along with the target material and finish. Identify which colors are brand-critical and which supporting colors can be handled more flexibly.

  • Approved color standard or controlled physical reference
  • Current artwork file and exact revision
  • Material and finish direction
  • Critical logo, safety or product-family colors
  • Proof approver and viewing conditions for final sign-off

Protect Readability While Pursuing the Color Target

A strong brand color can compete with barcode contrast, small warning text or a QR field. Keep product identification and scan requirements in the proof review. The best color result is not useful when it makes the label harder to read at the point of use.

FAQ

Questions Buyers Ask

Can label color match an existing product or package?

It can be discussed when the buyer provides a controlled reference, the intended material and finish, and a clear proof-approval route.

Why can the same color look different on two label materials?

Material surface, opacity, ink, finish and lighting can change the visual result. The final proof should use the intended construction.

Should color be approved before bulk production?

For brand-critical labels, a controlled proof review is a practical step before approving a bulk run.

Resolve a colour disagreement under recorded viewing conditions

If the label matches an approved panel in one room and differs in another, retain the same sample pair and record the lighting before changing the ink target. A photograph from each room may document the discussion, but camera processing and screens do not create a controlled physical colour comparison.

X-Rite describes metamerism as a pair appearing to match under one lighting condition and differing under another. A visual disagreement alone does not diagnose that mechanism. It is a reason to control the comparison and ask the responsible colour reviewer to assess it.

Reference and sampleItem, construction, finish and revision of each

Where the label must coordinate with a housing under more than one intended condition, put those conditions in the approval plan. Avoid guaranteeing that two materials match under every light. If measurements are required, agree the method and reporting conditions with the qualified reviewer rather than inventing a universal colour-difference limit.

Hold a revised target when reviewers are comparing different references or disagree about the evaluation conditions. Request one controlled reference, final label construction and the approved viewing requirements with the artwork.
