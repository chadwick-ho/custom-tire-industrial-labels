Old Tire Label Artwork: Review the GTIN Before Reusing a Discontinued Version

Published November 15, 2026

A buyer revives a saved tire label layout for a new model. The old model is discontinued, and its barcode remains in the design. Before quoting a repeat print run from that file, send the identifier back to the product-data owner for a reassignment decision.

Preserve the original product association

Identify the product and allocated GTIN associated with the archived artwork. Ask the owner to distinguish an actual continuation of the same trade item from a new product that merely uses its layout. Retain any stock, historical transactions and customer records linked to the old identity. Removing an item from a sales catalogue does not remove those relationships.

Have the GS1 identifier owner apply the applicable non-reuse and management rules, including any limited exceptions it considers relevant. The printer should receive the approved GTIN-to-model mapping and the active artwork revision. After that decision, compare the decoded proof and human-readable product data with the released mapping. A passing check digit and a successful scan cannot establish permission to reassign the number.

GS1’s explanation of GTIN non-reuse states the non-reuse rule and identifies limited exceptions. Ask the responsible identifier owner to review the exact product history.

Review pointEvidence to keep
ArchiveOriginal model, GTIN, artwork and retirement history.
New requestActual new product reference and the intended market workflow.
DecisionIdentifier-owner instruction and the applicable rule or approved exception.
Print proofActive mapping, decoded value and readable model information.

Before approving the order

Hold the barcode portion of the artwork if a new product still carries an unreviewed old number. A purely visual template can be reused after its product-specific fields are replaced and approved.

For tire barcode labels, send the new model and quantity list with the identifier owner’s approved barcode mapping and active artwork.
