Industrial Label Test Evidence: Check the Laboratory’s Accredited Scope

Published November 24, 2026

An equipment buyer needs an agreed label-material test and receives a laboratory proposal carrying an accreditation logo. Before paying for evidence intended to satisfy an accredited-test requirement, confirm that the particular activity is covered. An accredited laboratory can also offer services outside its accredited scope.

Match the proposed work to the scope, not just the institution

First ask the requirement owner whether accreditation is required for this decision and which evidence will be accepted. An early development trial may serve a different purpose from an order requiring a particular accredited report. Define that difference before commissioning work or comparing quoted test prices.

Request the actual performing laboratory’s legal name, location, accreditation body and identifying reference. Obtain the current scope from the accreditation body’s public record or ask for a confirmation route when the listing is not straightforward. Save the dated scope reference with the proposal.

Have the technical reviewer match the requested method, relevant material or activity category, and any stated limits against that scope. A familiar test title in a sales email may not identify the exact activity. Where the scope is flexible, ask the laboratory and accreditation body how coverage for the proposed work can be confirmed; do not interpret a short schedule beyond its stated meaning.

Ask whether any work will be subcontracted and which laboratory will issue the result. Confirm how the intended report will identify accredited activities and distinguish any results outside that coverage. A logo on the laboratory website does not answer these order-specific questions.

Keep sample suitability on a separate line in the review. Accreditation coverage does not establish that a generic film specimen represents the ink, adhesive and finish being purchased. Connect the agreed specimen to the actual proposed label construction and retain any preparation conditions.

Hong Kong Accreditation Service’s explanation of laboratory scope states that accreditation is test-specific and does not extend automatically to every service a laboratory offers. Use the relevant accreditation body’s current records for the actual laboratory; this article does not claim any CYJ laboratory accreditation.

What to include in the approval record

Review pointEvidence to keep
NeedAccepted evidence type and the owner’s accreditation requirement.
IdentityActual performing entity, site, accreditation reference and dated scope source.
CoverageRequested method and activity matched to scope, with ambiguities resolved by the responsible parties.
Report and sampleExpected reporting status, subcontracted work, specimen construction and release owner.

Before approving the order

Keep the accredited-test purchase pending if the coverage cannot be established. Ask the responsible reviewer whether a clarified proposal, another suitable laboratory or a different agreed evidence route is needed. Do not describe an activity outside scope as accredited to keep the schedule moving.

For a clearly identified exploratory trial that does not require accredited evidence, record its actual status and limits. Additional paperwork should support the buyer’s decision rather than imply an assurance that was never obtained.
