Labels on Molded Equipment Housings: Compare a Primer Step With a Different Adhesive

Published November 17, 2026

A durable equipment label sample sticks to a molded housing after a special preparation step. The buyer’s production team normally applies identification labels as soon as the housings reach assembly. Before ordering, decide whether to qualify and control that added step or investigate a construction intended for the normal line.

Compare repeatable application routes on the same housing

Record the housing reference, resin identification if known, surface finish, actual bonding area and handling before labeling. Ask the material or process owner whether a proposed primer is permitted for the specific construction and housing, with any required application controls. Keep its identity and approved process with the qualification record. Compare the complete proposed label-and-primer route with a suitable candidate construction under the normal preparation route. Use representative housings and the same sample geometry and practical exposure plan.

Ask the line owner who will perform, verify and record the extra step. Include any approved intervals, appearance criteria, rework limitations and in-process time in the comparison. Inspect the label edges, printed information and any surface effects expected to matter in service. If maintenance later needs to remove the label, include that use case in the evaluation. Replicate the chosen process for a production witness and record who approves deviations before release.

3M’s surface-preparation overview for plastics lists priming as a possible option where an adhesive needs added preparation. It does not establish compatibility for a particular equipment label.

What to include in the approval record

Review pointEvidence to keep
HousingProduced resin/finish reference, marked bonding area and how parts are handled.
Route AProposed label, approved preparation and primer process reference.
Route BCandidate label construction with the normal assembly preparation.
Release evidenceAttachment, appearance, use conditions, process owner and repeat trial results.

Before approving the order

Hold the order if a hand-prepared prototype is the only sample and production cannot reproduce it. Where the normal procedure already supports an approved construction under the intended conditions, keep that evidence with the reorder.

For equipment machinery labels, send the housing reference, surface photos, current preparation, service conditions, label dimensions and sample quantities.
