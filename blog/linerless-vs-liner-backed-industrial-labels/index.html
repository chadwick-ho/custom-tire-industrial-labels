Linerless vs Liner-Backed Labels: Check the Exact Printer Before Switching

Published November 5, 2026

A buyer wants to reduce discarded backing material at an industrial packing station and asks for the current labels without their liner. The intention is understandable, but the request describes a different media system rather than a small packing change.

Start with the exact printer and dispensing configuration, then assess an appropriate linerless construction and its application. Do not remove the backing from ordinary pressure-sensitive stock and treat it as a substitute. This guide explains a purchasing distinction; it does not announce a CYJ linerless product or confirm equipment compatibility.

The construction is different

Conventional liner-backed stock keeps the adhesive face separated from surrounding material by a release liner. Linerless media is designed to wind and dispense without that separate backing. The absence of a liner is therefore only one part of the specification.

Zebra's linerless printing guidance describes a release-coated top surface, adhesive backing and particular compatible device configurations. It explicitly limits compatibility to certain linerless models. Those details are an equipment-manufacturer example, not approval of any other printer or material.

Confirm the device variant, not just the family name

Obtain the installed configuration and approved media requirements from the equipment owner. A model family may have options that are not present on the actual device. Ask whether the proposed printer, media path, separation method and adhesive construction are approved together.

The ZD621 FAQ gives a useful example: its linerless variant has specific design features and is not described as a field-installable conversion. Do not generalize that product's arrangement into a modification procedure for another machine.

Keep the label's job in the review

A media option supported by a printer still needs to fit the intended surface and information lifetime. Describe whether the label identifies a carton, a temporary production item or durable equipment. State the actual exposure, removal requirement and data-readability task.

An option suited to a temporary direct-thermal workflow should not be assumed to replace a protected equipment-information label. Use the print-method guide to keep the printing question explicit. The chosen construction needs the evidence relevant to its actual application.

Use four approval questions

  1. Which exact equipment configuration and media specification are supported by the manufacturer?
  2. Which proposed construction is being assessed, and what technical information identifies it?
  3. What finished-label performance and data checks are needed on the actual substrate?
  4. What representative trial and named approval are required before replacing the current supply?

Request an equipment-owner assessment rather than copying cleaning, cutting or setup instructions from a general article. Operating and maintenance instructions belong to the actual machine and media combination.

Compare the whole change, including a decision to keep the liner

Record current liner handling, proposed equipment needs, confirmed media availability and any changes in label format or printing. Ask for order-specific costs instead of assuming that eliminating backing material guarantees a particular saving.

If the current approved label has a durable application the alternative cannot substantiate, retain the existing route while investigating another way to manage its backing waste. Any environmental claim should be tied to an actual scope and evidence; a generic claim about all linerless media does not establish a project's outcome.

Send the current label specification and the reason for the proposed change, including device configuration, surface, required information lifetime and quantities. Ask which options can actually be reviewed for the project rather than treating linerless as a drop-in material name.
