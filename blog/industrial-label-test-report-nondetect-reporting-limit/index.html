Industrial Label Test Reports: Read the Limit Behind “Not Detected”

Published November 24, 2026

A quality buyer receives a material report marked ND for a proposed equipment-label construction. The purchasing specification contains a numerical threshold. Before approving the material, determine what ND means in that report and whether the result can support that particular decision.

Compare the reporting basis with the actual requirement

Ask the laboratory or report issuer to define its notation. Detection limits, quantitation limits and reporting limits are not interchangeable headings. Record which limit applies to the stated result, the units and any qualification. Do not silently replace ND with zero in a spreadsheet or declaration.

Use a fictional arithmetic example, unrelated to any regulation: suppose a buyer requires no more than 50 mg/kg of a specified analyte in a defined material, while the report states only less than 100 mg/kg on the same basis. A value below 100 could still be above 50. That statement alone cannot establish the buyer’s threshold. It also does not prove that the threshold was exceeded.

Before comparing numbers, have the responsible technical reviewer confirm the analyte or group definition, test method, sample preparation, material basis and units match the requirement. Evidence for one adhesive or face material should not expand silently to a finished assembly. A lower-looking number on a different basis may answer a different question.

If the evidence is insufficient, ask the reviewer and laboratory what additional work is appropriate and whether a suitably sensitive method is available for the actual material. Do not order an unrelated environmental method simply because its reporting limit is small. Ask about the proposed specimen, scope, cost and timing before making the test a production dependency.

California Water Boards’ Integrated Report FAQ explains in its water-data context why nondetect is not necessarily zero. That supports the notation distinction only; it does not prescribe a method, threshold or compliance decision for label materials.

What to include in the approval record

Review pointEvidence to keep
RequirementDefined analyte, threshold, units, material basis and responsible reviewer.
Report identityIssuer, report revision, specimen and link to the proposed construction.
Result meaningLaboratory definition of ND, relevant limit and any qualifiers.
DecisionReviewer’s documented conclusion or request for additional suitable evidence.

Before approving the order

Keep material approval pending if the reviewer cannot connect the result and its reporting capability to the specified requirement. Do not change the buyer’s threshold to match an available report. This check is useful for a quantitative substance requirement; it cannot establish a broad “free of everything” claim from a limited analyte list.

For equipment machinery labels, send the stated material requirement, proposed construction, available report with its legend and quantities so the evidence gap can be identified.
