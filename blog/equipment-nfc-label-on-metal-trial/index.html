NFC Labels on Metal Equipment: Approve the Installed Read

Published November 12, 2026

The maintenance team wants technicians to tap a tag on a painted metal enclosure. A loose sample works at the desk. Buying needs a second result with the actual tag construction mounted at the intended touchpoint.

Specify the housing and the installed reading position

Mark the proposed location on a photo or drawing. Include the base metal, coating, access angle and nearby parts that will be present during use. List the supported phone or handheld models and expected action after a read. Confirm whether an optical code or printed ID remains necessary for staff who cannot use the tap workflow.

Ask the tag supplier to identify which offered construction is designed for the mounting environment. A backing intended for metal may change the thickness and adhesive contact, so check both enclosure clearance and fit. Hold the data constant across samples and evaluate the installed configuration separately from the loose demonstration.

STMicroelectronics’ NFC touchpoint guide shows a ferrite-backed example for metal and recommends tests with representative phones in the final mounted conditions. Confirm the actual sample specification and reader list with the responsible owners.

Review pointEvidence to record before ordering
MountingActual housing material, coating, nearby hardware and designated touchpoint.
ConstructionSupplier sample reference, thickness, backing and placement method.
Observed resultSupported device, case or cover used, correct asset record and readable fallback.

When to release the order

Hold when the only result is a loose-tag test or the intended devices disagree. The asset-system owner should approve the supported workflow and the equipment owner should approve mounting. If an ordinary optical code already meets the reading and access requirements, compare it before adding an electronic inlay.
