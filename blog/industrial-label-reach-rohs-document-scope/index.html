REACH and RoHS Label Requests: Specify the Construction and Document Scope

Published November 6, 2026

An equipment OEM asks purchasing for REACH and RoHS documents for its new serial-number label. The supplier sends a test report for a white film. The order also includes adhesive, coloured printing and a protective finish. The film report may be relevant, but its relationship to the complete supplied label is unresolved.

Request documents for an identified construction and a clearly stated customer requirement. Keep REACH and RoHS as separate review questions. A general compliance badge or an unqualified REACH free sentence does not describe which obligations, substances, components or evidence have been considered.

Start with the requirement owner

The European Commission describes RoHS as restrictions on hazardous substances in electrical and electronic equipment. ECHA's supply-chain guidance addresses communication about Candidate List substances in articles. These are different frameworks; one supplier statement should not silently be substituted for the other.

Have the OEM's responsible compliance reviewer define the applicable requirement, market, relevant legal references and requested declaration or evidence format. This article organizes a label-supply inquiry. It does not determine legal applicability, exemptions or the compliance of the assembled equipment.

Connect every document to the supplied item

Record fieldWhat to ask
ConstructionWhich face, adhesive, printing and finish does the statement cover?
RequirementWhich regime, customer specification and reference date are addressed?
Evidence basisIs this a declaration, analytical report or another assessment?
IdentityCan its item, sample and revision be linked to the offer?
LimitsWhich components, substances or conditions are outside its scope?
ReviewWho accepts the evidence and what remains outstanding?

Do not require every document to be the same type. A supplier declaration and a laboratory report serve different roles. Have the reviewer decide what evidence is adequate for the stated requirement. The word tested should identify what was tested and to what basis; it should not expand a sample's result to unrelated constructions.

Keep dates and changing lists visible

Where a statement refers to the REACH Candidate List, record the list reference or date it addresses. ECHA maintains the official list. Do not treat a year printed on a familiar declaration as proof that subsequent changes were reviewed. Ask the supplier and requirement owner how currency is established for this order.

For RoHS, ask the responsible reviewer to identify the applicable material assessment and any claimed exemption basis when relevant. The RoHS Directive uses a homogeneous-material basis for its concentration limits. A finished label's overall weight should not be used casually to reinterpret material-specific evidence. Obtain the required technical assessment instead of performing unsupported compliance arithmetic in the quotation.

Control substitutions after the document review

Artwork approval and materials-document acceptance should refer to the same released construction. If an adhesive, ink, film or protective layer changes, ask which earlier statements are affected and what further review is required. An unchanged asset number does not mean the material supply is unchanged.

Keep the document reference in the purchase specification and connect delivered item identification to it. Avoid printing an environmental or regulatory mark on the label merely because a document was supplied. Any proposed buyer-facing wording needs its own authorized approval.

What to send with the enquiry

For equipment identification labels, send a construction-and-documents brief with the OEM requirement, intended market, available form or report references, complete proposed construction, surface, exposure, artwork, size and quantities. Ask which documents are available and list missing evidence explicitly. Keep approval pending when the responsible reviewer cannot connect the evidence to the offer.
