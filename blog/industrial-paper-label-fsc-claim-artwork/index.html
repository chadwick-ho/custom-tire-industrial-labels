FSC Wording on a Paper Label: Identify the Claim Before Adding the Logo

Published November 6, 2026

An industrial buyer asks for paper labels with responsible-source documentation. The artwork arrives with an FSC logo copied from an earlier packaging job. Nobody has established which product the claim describes, which certified supply route applies, or who may approve the graphic.

Separate the material purchase from the trademark decision. Asking for evidence about paper does not by itself authorize adding an on-product label to new artwork. Start with the intended claim, the exact supplied item and the responsible approval route.

Name the product covered by the claim

A pressure-sensitive supply may include a paper face, adhesive, release liner and shipping packaging. The label may later be attached to a piece of equipment. Specify whether the proposed wording concerns the label stock, a packaging component or another defined product. Do not let a paper-source statement imply certification of the equipment.

FSC's on-product guidance directs buyers to check the final supplier's certification and describes the certified supplier's role in obtaining approved graphics. It also distinguishes the FSC label types. Follow the applicable current FSC requirements and responsible certification route; do not choose a graphic merely because it resembles the paper colour.

Use a claim-to-artwork record

DecisionRecord neededDo not infer
Claim subjectExact item or component describedThat every component or attached product is covered
Supply basisRelevant supplier, product scope and order documentationThat a logo on a website qualifies every order
GraphicAuthorized label file and applicable approvalThat a copied or redrawn logo is approved
ReleaseApproved version and person responsibleThat ordinary colour proofing establishes claim eligibility

These are review fields, not findings about CYJ certification. This article does not establish a certified chain of custody or authorize a mark on any proposed supply. Obtain the specific supporting information before describing an order as eligible.

Ask for evidence before finalising the design

Identify the actual supplier and ask how the offered product and claim are documented through the order. Check relevant certification details through the official route. Retain the applicable scope and dated evidence, rather than relying on a cropped certificate with no link to the supplied product.

Have the authorized party supply the correct graphic and approval instructions. A designer should not invent a license code, combine elements from different labels or turn a material inquiry into an approved trademark claim. Keep correspondence resolving those questions with the released artwork.

Do not exchange one environmental claim for another

Responsible-source information, recycled content and recyclability answer different questions. Evidence for one should not be recycled into an unrelated statement. The component and recycling-route guide deals with a separate end-of-use review.

A paper face may also be a poor choice for the buyer's required service conditions. Evaluate readable information, attachment, moisture and handling for the actual industrial application. A supported sourcing claim does not establish outdoor life, cleaner resistance or printer compatibility.

Hold unsupported artwork while the purchase review continues

If the claim subject, supply route or graphic approval is unresolved, keep the mark off the production release. The buyer can continue discussing neutral artwork and material options while the authorized reviewer obtains the missing evidence. Do not substitute a different environmental symbol simply to fill the space.

For industrial paper-label enquiries, send one material-and-claim brief with the exact proposed wording, component it describes, available supplier documents, application conditions, dimensions, artwork and quantities. Ask what documented options and approval route are available for that project. Source checked November 6, 2026.
