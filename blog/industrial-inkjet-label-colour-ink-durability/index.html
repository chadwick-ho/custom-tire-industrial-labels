Inkjet Industrial Labels: Check the Colour Inks, Not Only the Black Text

Published November 6, 2026

A maintenance buyer trials an inkjet label with a black asset number. The sample looks satisfactory after the site's agreed handling check. The production design later adds coloured status information, and the same trial is cited as approval for the whole label. That conclusion leaves the colour printing unevaluated.

Identify the inks used by each information element and the exact coated stock before deciding what a sample proves. Printer technology, film type and a waterproof-material description do not by themselves establish the performance of every printed colour. This review concerns a proposed local printing process; it does not promise that CYJ supplies a qualified inkjet stock.

Record the actual ink system

Avery's inkjet guidance points out that some devices combine pigment black ink with dye-based colour inks. Its material guidance also describes performance differences for a particular film depending on the printing system. Those product examples support checking the actual combination, not a general promise for every pigment ink or synthetic label.

Obtain the installed device model and the actual cartridge references. Do not infer the complete ink set from the black cartridge description. Ask the equipment owner to confirm permitted consumables and media; an industrial-label article is not an instruction to substitute inks or load unapproved stock.

Make the sample represent the important information

Use the production artwork, including coloured information that matters to the task. If colour indicates status, pair it with approved readable wording so colour alone does not silently become the only interpretation route. Keep warning content under the responsible content owner's approval.

Black ID and serialActual field, ink reference and source dataLegibility and exact identity
Coloured text or statusActual colour channels and their meaningWhether meaning remains unambiguous
Barcode or QRProduction symbol and intended readerDecode and business-record match
Label attachmentSurface, adhesive and preparationAgreed attachment observations

A black number remaining readable is a valid observation about that sample. It does not establish that a coloured word survived, that the adhesive remained acceptable, or that a different coating will behave the same way. Record separate outcomes instead of giving the entire label one unexplained pass.

Specify exposure and the first handling step

Tell the supplier what contacts the printed face, how often, for how long and under which conditions. Include the interval between printing and the first handling operation, using the selected supplier's instructions and the site's approved process. Avoid inventing a universal drying time.

A dry handling check, a water splash and contact with a named cleaner answer different questions. Have the responsible reviewer choose a controlled evaluation representing the intended use, including the finished coating or protection layer. Keep print preparation and acceptance conditions in the record. Do not turn an informal wipe into a chemical-resistance certification.

Choose a supply route after the evidence review

Local inkjet output may suit a workflow needing approved colour information assigned on site. Fully preprinted labels may remove an unnecessary local printing step when all information is known beforehand. Thermal transfer is another process with its own media and ribbon review. None is automatically the right route merely because a printer is available.

Pause production approval if the colour inks, stock or required exposure remain unidentified. For industrial labels, send a printed-system brief containing the device and cartridges, stock reference, complete artwork, surface, actual contacts, required readable lifetime and quantities. Ask which options and evidence are available for that exact request. Sources checked November 6, 2026.
