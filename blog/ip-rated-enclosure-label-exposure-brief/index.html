Labels for IP-Rated Equipment: Specify the Label’s Actual Exposure

Published November 15, 2026

An equipment buyer forwards an enclosure specification and asks for a label with the same IP rating. The identification label sits outside the protected enclosure. Before comparing materials, ask the equipment owner what label function and exposure the purchase requirement is intended to cover.

Identify the tested object and the marking task

Find the reference that establishes the enclosure requirement and ask the responsible owner whether the proposed label is simply identification or part of a sealing or other assessed assembly. Mark its installed position on a drawing. If it crosses an access seam, seal or operating feature, have the equipment designer review that arrangement before a label specification is released.

For the label itself, describe contact with water, dust, cleaners or handling at that position. Define what must remain attached and legible and when an operator must read or scan it. Request evidence for the complete printed construction on the actual substrate. The equipment owner should separately determine whether its assembly needs evaluation after the label is installed or changed.

IEC’s published scope for IEC 60529 identifies the IP Code as degrees of protection provided by enclosures. Use the equipment owner’s specification to determine the actual assessment and a suitable label qualification.

Review pointEvidence to keep
ReferenceEnclosure requirement, tested object and responsible equipment owner.
PlacementLabel position and any relationship to seams, seals or controls.
Label conditionsActual environmental contact, attachment and reading requirements.
AcceptanceConstruction-specific label witness and any required assembly review.

Before approving the order

Hold an unsupported rating statement on the quotation or artwork. Ask the owner to define the exposure evidence it actually needs. A proven label construction still needs the approved placement and content for this equipment.

Related reading includes the waterproof label checklist, the immersion and reading witness, the enclosure placement guide.

For electronics electrical labels, send the enclosure requirement, marked label position, surface and actual exposure and reading conditions.
