BS 5609 Label Documents: Check the Edition, Sections and Printed System

Published November 6, 2026

A chemical-container label quotation says BS 5609. The attached document names a material, while the proposed order will use different printing and a plastic drum. Procurement needs to know whether the evidence supports the actual supply, rather than treating the standard number as a complete answer.

Ask for the standard edition, covered sections, tested construction, print system and surfaces. Have the buyer's responsible technical and dangerous-goods reviewers decide which requirements apply to the shipment. This guide helps organize that evidence; it does not declare any CYJ label certified or decide transport compliance from an inquiry.

Read the edition and section scope together

BSI identifies BS 5609:2024 as the current published specification for printed pressure-sensitive marine-use labels and label stock. UL Solutions explains that section 2 addresses face-stock and adhesive performance on aluminium and HDPE surfaces, while section 3 concerns printing durability. Its summary also notes that the 2024 revision permits section 3 evaluation without prior section 2 compliance.

Therefore, a reference to section 3 alone should not silently become a statement that section 2 was also covered. Equally, a base-material document should not be presented as evidence for any ink subsequently printed on that material. Ask what the actual document establishes and what remains unassessed.

The table is a purchasing record, not a new test method. Obtain the full relevant documentation and its limitations. Where the order differs, describe the difference to the qualified reviewer rather than writing equivalent beside it without a basis.

Keep an older report visible as an older report

An existing document may cite an earlier edition. Record that accurately and ask the customer or responsible assessor whether it satisfies the applicable specification or needs further evaluation. Do not rewrite its date or edition in a quotation. A new purchase date does not update the underlying test scope.

UL's current summary describes additional test-surface provisions and changes to testing in the 2024 revision. Use it as a reason to review applicability, not as a substitute for the licensed standard or an assessor's decision. Do not invent a transition deadline or declare every older document invalid from this article.

Durability approval does not supply the hazard information

The buyer still needs approved chemical identity, warning content, languages, symbols and variable fields. A label-durability record does not classify a substance or establish the correct content for every market and shipment. Check content and construction through their separate owners before releasing artwork.

Likewise, a readable barcode requires its own approved data and applicable scan or verification checks. Retain the released artwork and construction references with the documentary review so a later ink, finish or stock change can be assessed.

For chemical-container labels, submit a marine-use evidence brief with the exact buyer specification, container surface, available reports, proposed printing system, approved content, size, exposure and quantity. Ask what documented supply is available and leave unsupported certification claims on hold. Sources checked November 6, 2026.
