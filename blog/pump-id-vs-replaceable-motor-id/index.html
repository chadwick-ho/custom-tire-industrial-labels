Pump IDs vs Replaceable Motor IDs

A pump skid can stay in place when its motor goes to the repair shop. If the only number is on the motor, the pump appears to disappear with it. If the number is only on the frame, the removed motor loses its own history.

Quick Answer: Name the Assembly and the Removable Part

Give the fixed installation or pump its buyer-approved ID and give a removable motor its own component ID when the maintenance system tracks it independently. Keep the relationship between them in the equipment record, not by printing the pump number permanently onto every replacement motor. Manufacturer plates and ratings must remain visible and unchanged. Grundfos provides pump product-number and spare-parts lookup guidance; its examples illustrate why approved equipment identity matters, not a universal label-placement rule.

An Illustrative Motor Swap

Imagine a workshop removes motor M-318 from pump P-204 and installs a spare. This is a composite example, not a maintenance record. A label saying only P-204 on the motor makes the removed unit look like the pump, while a frame plate saying only M-318 becomes wrong as soon as the spare is fitted. Separate IDs let the mechanic record which motor was installed at which pump and when, without changing the fixed pump identity.

Place Labels on the Right Physical Objects

The installation or pump ID belongs on a stable visible part approved by the equipment owner. The motor ID belongs on a part that will travel with that motor if it is removed. Keep labels away from cooling openings, rotating parts, hot zones, original nameplates and wiring access. Photograph the installed and removed views before choosing dimensions. Do not use a fictional sample image to approve an electrical or mechanical marking.

Map the Swap in the Record

A work order should link pump ID, outgoing motor ID, incoming motor ID and the authorized person who performed the exchange. A code on either label is only a lookup key. The buyer should check that the printed text and encoded data resolve to the correct record after a change, including when network access is unavailable. Use a finished-size sample on representative painted or textured metal.

  • PUMP: fixed location or machine identity.
  • MOTOR: physical component identity that travels with it.
  • PAIRING: time-bounded relationship in the controlled record.
  • SOURCE: original manufacturer plate left uncovered.
  • PROOF: installed and removed read points.

What to Send for a Quote

Supply photographs of the pump body, skid and motor housing, approved ID schema, label dimensions, surface finish, expected oil and cleaning exposure, quantity and scan devices. Say which components are likely to be swapped. Request two separate sample labels and an example pairing record with fictional numbers. A print supplier can produce the identifiers; the owner must approve the equipment mapping.

FAQ

Questions Buyers Ask

Can a replacement motor inherit the removed motor ID?

Not when each physical motor has its own tracked history. Update the pairing record and keep IDs attached to their respective units.

Does an asset label replace the manufacturer nameplate?

No. A buyer asset ID is an additional lookup aid and should not cover or rewrite original rating information.
