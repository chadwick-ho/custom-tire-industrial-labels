Tire Label Order Quantities: Agree Overruns and Shortfalls by SKU

Published November 3, 2026

You need labels for two tire sizes. The delivery total matches the purchase order, but one size has spare rolls while the other cannot cover the packing job. A correct overall count has hidden a shortage where the labels are actually needed.

For a multiple-SKU tire-label purchase, agree the required quantity and any permitted variation for each SKU before production. Record how delivered pieces will be counted, how extras are charged or handled, and what happens if a line is short. There is no default overrun percentage in this guide, and no automatic right to supply or bill extra labels.

Separate the packing requirement from the print quantity

The planner knows how many tires must be labelled. The converter needs an approved production quantity. Receiving needs a count it can reconcile, while finance needs a billing basis. Put those purposes beside their numbers so a spare allowance is not silently added twice.

Define whether the requested amount means finished labels delivered, labels available for application, or a production quantity that includes separately identified samples or setup material. For a tire-size label, extra stock for one size cannot replace missing stock for another. For serialized labels, even a physically identical replacement may need a different data instruction.

The existing multiple-SKU ordering guide covers the artwork-to-roll relationship. This additional check concerns the quantity each row is allowed to deliver and the commercial treatment of a difference.

A matched total can still leave the line short

Consider an illustrative order with 1,000 labels each of fictional SKU A and SKU B. A delivery containing 1,050 of A and 950 of B still totals 2,000. If both packing jobs need 1,000 labels, B is 50 short. This arithmetic is a teaching example, not a recommended tolerance or an actual shipment.

Reconcile every row against its own agreed requirement. A group-level allowance should not be presumed to permit a shortage on a particular SKU. If the buyer can accept variation, identify the limits, decision owner and effect on the packing plan explicitly. If an exact count is required, ask whether the supplier can quote and supply that requirement before releasing the order.

Use a quantity agreement with five fields

  1. Required amount: SKU, artwork revision, requested finished-label quantity and purpose of any spare allocation.
  2. Permitted delivery range: agreed lower and upper bounds per SKU, or an explicit exact-quantity requirement. Leave unagreed values marked pending.
  3. Count basis: labels per roll, partial-roll reporting, excluded material and which record receiving checks. Carton count alone does not establish label count.
  4. Billing basis: what the quoted amount covers, whether permitted extras change the invoice, and who must approve a change. Keep currency and offer revision with the decision.
  5. Exception action: whom to notify about a shortfall, whether to hold an affected SKU, and how any agreed replenishment or other resolution will be recorded.

The custom-order policy treats specifications as project-specific. Therefore obtain a written order-specific agreement rather than treating this worksheet as CYJ's standard tolerance, price or replacement policy.

Keep spare pieces separate from spare identities

Repeated SKU labels and individually serialized labels need different data checks. If every label carries a unique identity, agree the authorized identifier range, how rejected pieces are recorded and what happens to unused identifiers. Buying more physical pieces is not permission to invent, reuse or activate identifiers.

Scantrust's handling guidance describes surplus and rejected-code controls in its own secure-code system. That is a useful example of why physical counts and identity status need coordination; its particular code workflow and quantity examples do not establish rules for a CYJ order.

Close the difference before using the affected stock

At receipt, preserve the roll and carton identifiers and record actual quantities by SKU. If the count falls outside the agreed range, ask the buyer's responsible owner and supplier to reconcile it. Do not hide a shortfall by moving quantities between rows. If the concern is excluded labels near a splice, use the separate splice and usable-quantity guide.

If demand changes after printing, review the quantity and revision together. More labels are useful only while their tire information and intended use remain current. Keep the original agreement and any acknowledged change with the receiving record.

Send a tire-label quantity brief with quantities per SKU, approved artwork, required usable amount, proposed spare allowance, roll format and any exact-count constraint. Ask for the quantity and billing basis to be confirmed with the quotation.
