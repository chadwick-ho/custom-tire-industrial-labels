Automatic Tire Label Rolls: The Applicator Needs More Than Artwork

A beautiful tire label can still stop a line if the liner, gap or winding direction does not suit the machine. Build the roll specification from the actual applicator manual and a line sample.

Quick Answer: Send the Machine Specification

The applicator must feed, detect, peel and place each label consistently. Capture the machine make and model, label width and pitch, gap or mark sensor method, liner width and type, core inside diameter, maximum roll outside diameter, winding direction and label orientation on the web. Zebra documentation illustrates how media sensors may rely on gaps, notches or black marks, but a tire applicator may have different hardware. Its own manual and a production trial govern the purchase.

An Illustrative Line Stop

Imagine a purchasing team sending only a PDF of the tire graphic and the desired label size. The delivered roll fits on the spindle, but the sensing mark is on the wrong side of the liner. Operators can hand-peel a few labels, yet the automatic feed misses positions. This is a composite example, not a report of a real line. The missing information is not another artwork revision; it is a signed media drawing and a short trial with the intended roll construction.

Specify the Web, Not Just the Label

Ask the equipment owner for a current approved media sample. Measure the label after die cutting, then record the web pitch, edge margin and leading-edge orientation as the machine sees them. Confirm whether the sensor reads through the gap, sees a printed back mark or follows another registration method. Record label face-out or face-in, left or right unwind and the direction of text as the roll enters the applicator. Treat all dimensions as project values, not generic tire-industry defaults.

  • FINISHED LABEL: width, length, corner shape and print position.
  • LINER: width, release characteristics and any back mark.
  • PITCH: label plus gap measured along feed direction.
  • ROLL: core, outer diameter, winding and splice policy.
  • APPLICATION: tire position, speed, pressure and contact area.

Do a Representative Application Trial

Run a small approved roll on the actual dispenser with the intended tire range. Look for missed picks, liner breaks, label curl, inaccurate placement and readable data after application. Some failures come from media geometry; others from the applicator setting or tire surface. Do not infer adhesive performance from clean release at the peel plate. Check adhesion separately on representative tread surfaces under the real application conditions. Record machine settings with the approved roll version so a reorder is not a new experiment.

Quote Brief and Change Control

Send the applicator model, roll drawing, approved sample, face material and adhesive requirement, printed artwork, variable-data file, tire surface photos, target quantity and scan method. State which fields may change by SKU and which dimensions must stay fixed for the machine. Ask for a physical proof and approved media specification before volume production. A change in liner, gap or core is a process change even if the label artwork remains identical.

FAQ

Questions Buyers Ask

Is core diameter enough to specify an automatic roll?

No. Liner, sensing method, pitch, orientation, outside diameter and machine settings matter too.

Can a hand-applied sample prove line performance?

No. Test a representative roll on the intended applicator and tire surface.

Approve detection on the finished label and liner

A roll that changes from opaque to transparent film may need a new detection review even when its size and artwork stay the same. Obtain the exact sensor model and sensing method from the equipment owner. Do not infer compatibility from the printer or applicator brand alone.

SICK's UFS overview describes an ultrasonic sensor that distinguishes the label-plus-carrier region from carrier alone by signal attenuation. It is a model-specific example of a detection method suited to certain optically challenging labels. It does not qualify another sensor, a complete applicator or any CYJ construction.

Before ordering, have the equipment owner assess the actual face material, adhesive, liner, artwork coverage and registration geometry as one finished web. If a detection mark or revised gap is proposed, confirm the required face, position and dimensions in a controlled drawing rather than adding a generic black rectangle.

Agree a representative finished-roll trial and retain the sample identity, authorized configuration and observations. Keep detection, dispensing, tire adhesion and barcode-data checks separate. A detectable gap does not prove that the label releases correctly or identifies the intended tire.
