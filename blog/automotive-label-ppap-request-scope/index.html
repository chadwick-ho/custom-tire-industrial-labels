An Automotive Label Order Requests PPAP: Define the Submission Before Quoting

Published November 7, 2026

An automotive buyer asks for custom identification labels and adds PPAP required to the purchase enquiry. The print file is available, but nobody has supplied the customer-specific submission requirements. A price and a physical sample cannot establish what the requested approval package will include.

Resolve the submission scope before accepting a delivery promise or treating the request as a standard certificate. This is a conditional buying guide for a project where the customer expressly requires PPAP. It does not state that every tire label needs PPAP or that CYJ has a verified PPAP service or customer approval.

Ask which approval is being requested

AIAG publishes the Production Part Approval Process manual. Its public terminology guide describes PPAP as evidence that customer engineering requirements are understood and the production process can consistently meet them during an actual run. That purpose is broader than approving the appearance of one printed label.

Have the authorized customer quality representative identify the applicable requirements, revisions and submission expectations. Do not infer a submission level, production-run quantity, waiver or acceptance from a generic online checklist. Use the current licensed requirements and the customer's authorized instructions where the project calls for them.

Agree an enquiry-stage responsibility map

RecordQuestion to close
Customer requirementWhich requirement and revision govern this label item?
Design responsibilityWho owns the drawing, information and acceptance characteristics?
Submission scopeWhat evidence is requested, retained or supplied, under which instructions?
Process identityWhich intended construction, production route and site are in scope?
Evidence ownershipWho can generate, review and authorize each required result?
Release and timingWhat decision permits which subsequent supply?

This map is a purchasing aid, not a replacement for PPAP requirements or a complete submission checklist. Mark a capability or evidence item pending when it has not been confirmed. Do not fill missing records with invented measurements, borrowed certificates or another item's approval.

Quote the actual work and dependencies

Separate ordinary artwork preparation, physical samples, any agreed evaluation and the proposed documentation work. Ask what each deliverable represents, what information it needs and who accepts it. A supplier should confirm whether it can support the requested scope before its price or schedule is treated as a commitment.

Identify customer decisions that block later work: a missing drawing revision, unresolved construction, undefined characteristic or absent approval owner. A requested ship date does not resolve those dependencies. Keep production authorization, submission review and shipment instructions explicit rather than compressing them into sample approved.

Keep later receiving checks separate

An approval package and the acceptance of a delivered lot answer different questions. Use the lot-acceptance guide to define the actual supply, check and disposition at receipt. A shipment conformance statement also has its own scope; it should not be renamed PPAP evidence without a basis.

If the agreed construction or production route changes, return the change to the customer-approved process before assuming earlier approval still applies. The print supplier should not invent universal notification or resubmission rules for another customer's program.

For automotive industrial-label enquiries, send a submission-scope brief with the authorized requirement, controlled drawing, proposed construction, required evidence, responsible reviewers, quantities and approval-dependent dates. Ask what can actually be supported; hold an unsupported submission promise. Sources checked November 7, 2026.
