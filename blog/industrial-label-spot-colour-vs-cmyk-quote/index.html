A Pantone Reference on a Label Quote: Spot Ink or a CMYK Simulation?

Published November 7, 2026

The equipment-label artwork names a Pantone colour. One supplier proposes a dedicated spot ink; another proposes a process-colour simulation. The quotes appear to specify the same blue, so purchasing compares only their totals. The colour target is shared, but the proposed production routes have not been made equivalent.

Record the reference and the production colour mode separately. A colour number communicates a target; it does not, by itself, confirm that an offer includes a dedicated ink, particular separation or accepted match on the finished label. Ask for that answer before approving price or artwork.

Understand what the reference can show

Pantone describes its Color Bridge guide as a side-by-side comparison of a solid colour and its closest CMYK simulation. That distinction makes simulation explicit. It is not a guarantee that a converted design on another stock, printer or finish will match the solid reference.

Supply the precise reference, its intended use and any approved physical sample. Do not send several screenshots and ask the converter to infer the controlling target. Where a colour is safety-related or part of a technical requirement, have its responsible owner define the applicable acceptance basis.

Put the proposed mode in the quotation

FieldQuestion to resolve
TargetWhich controlled colour reference and revision apply?
Production modeDedicated spot ink, process simulation or another confirmed route?
ConstructionWhich face stock, opacity and final finish are included?
EvidenceWhich proof or printed sample will represent the offer?
AcceptanceWho approves the result, under which agreed conditions?
ChargesWhich preparation, ink or repeat-run items are included?

A supplier may offer alternatives with different cost or production implications. Keep each alternative identifiable rather than accepting a quotation headed colour matched when the proposed mode is still unresolved. This article does not establish available CYJ ink stations or promise either printing route.

Review the actual separation and finished sample

Have the artwork and prepress owners confirm how the target is represented in the released output. A named spot swatch in an editable file does not prove that the returned production output preserves that treatment. Equally, a screen showing the desired colour does not establish the printed result.

Review an appropriate sample on the offered construction with the agreed final finish. The colour-matching guide covers the controlled reference and viewing conditions. Here the additional record is the production mode attached to that approval. Do not transfer approval from an uncoated reference to a different finished film without reviewing applicability.

Keep machine-readable information separate

A brand-colour decision should not silently recolour barcode bars or their surrounding area. Identify which objects follow the brand target and which retain the independently approved code construction. Our barcode-colour review explains why visual branding and optical capture need different checks.

If the proposed simulation does not satisfy the agreed colour requirement, ask about a supported alternative or an owner-approved revised target. Do not label an unaccepted result close enough to protect a quoted price. Conversely, a buyer who accepts the simulated result may not need an unnecessary dedicated-ink requirement.

For industrial label printing, send a colour-mode brief with the reference, marked artwork objects, proposed spot or process requirement, material and finish, acceptance route and quantity by version. Resolve the mode and its evidence before comparing quotations as equivalent. Source checked November 7, 2026.
