Reused Carton Labels: Remove Old Barcodes Before the Next Shipment

Published November 2, 2026

A usable carton returns to the packing bench with yesterday's carrier label on one side and an item label on another. The next shipment needs a new address and tracking code. If an old shipping barcode remains visible, the next person or automated reader may encounter more than one possible code. The packing team needs a repeatable decision, not just a larger new label.

Before reuse, identify each existing mark, remove or fully obscure obsolete shipment information, and leave the current approved shipment label clearly visible. Retain or replace item, lot and handling information according to its owner. Check the current carrier rules and your site's process for the actual shipment.

Identify the purpose before covering a mark

Not every barcode on a carton belongs to the same job. A carrier code directs a particular shipment; an item or lot label may identify the contents; a return authorization may point to a separate record. Covering all of them blindly can remove information the receiver needs.

FedEx Office's shipping reference instructs its staff to remove old labels or cross out their barcodes. That guidance is specific to its shipping process; check the applicable carrier's current rules and any handling or product requirements for your package.

Build a packing-station check

  1. Inspect every face. Find old carrier labels, returns labels, partially torn codes and overlapping labels. Record what is required to stay, including any relevant handling information.
  2. Remove or cover the obsolete shipment code. Follow the applicable carrier instructions. If using a covering label, choose a size and construction that fully obscures the obsolete information and stays attached to the actual carton surface. Verify this on the finished carton; do not assume an untested material is opaque.
  3. Check the carton itself. If removing a label damages the carton, exposes a seam or compromises closure, set it aside for the packing owner to assess. The label is not a repair method for unsuitable packaging.
  4. Apply the current shipment label. Follow carrier instructions for a flat, visible position. Keep the new barcode clear of seams, folds and obstructions. FedEx advises against tape over its shipping barcode because reflections can interfere with scanning; another carrier's requirements may differ.
  5. Review at the handoff. Confirm the current address and identifier against the shipment record, check that obsolete codes cannot be mistaken for the current one, and inspect from the actual handling side. A successful scan does not prove the encoded shipment data belongs to the right order.

Choose a removal or covering method that fits the operation

Removal may work when the old label and carton allow a clean result. Where removal tears the surface or leaves unreadable fragments, an approved opaque cover may be worth trialing. Ask who decides which option is permitted, which material is used, and how staff check the result. Do not cover safety, regulatory or handling information without the responsible owner's instruction.

For a returnable plastic container, the problem can be different: its permanent asset identifier may need to survive many trips. See reusable tote ID versus trip labels for that decision. This guide concerns a carton being prepared for a new shipment.

What to include when ordering covering labels

Send photos of representative cartons and the old labels, the dimensions to cover, surface condition, storage and handling environment, quantity, application method and expected time on the carton. State whether printing or variable data is required. Ask for a representative sample and check coverage, adhesion and any impact on required markings before placing a production order.
