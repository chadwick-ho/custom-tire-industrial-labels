A Label Colour Tolerance Needs the Delta E Equation

Published November 22, 2026

A purchasing team receives two equipment-label proof reports. Each says Delta E, but only one names the equation. Ranking the offers by the smaller number could compare different calculations. Before accepting a colour guarantee or rejecting a sample, ask the colour reviewer to specify the calculation and the conditions attached to the agreed limit.

Treat the equation as part of the requirement

Obtain one controlled reference and identify the exact label construction, print and finish represented by each sample. Ask for the complete reported metric, such as CIE76 or CIEDE2000, including relevant calculation settings. A bare number does not identify the comparison being made. Do not reuse the same numerical limit under a different equation without the requirement owner’s review.

The measurement record also needs the instrument and setup, illuminant and observer settings used for the calculation, backing and measurement locations where relevant. Keep those conditions consistent with the approved reference. For a label with several critical colours, name the actual patches or regions to evaluate rather than reporting one unexplained average for the whole design.

Have the reviewer define how individual readings, multiple positions and repeat measurements affect release. A mean can conceal a failed critical region if the contract only records the average. The appropriate limit belongs to the customer’s application and agreement; this article does not supply a universal acceptable number. Retain visual approval under the agreed viewing conditions alongside measurements where the project needs both.

HunterLab’s explanation of colour-difference equations distinguishes CIE76, CIE94 and CIEDE2000. It explains why the equation name matters; it does not set an industrial-label purchasing tolerance.

What to include in the approval record

Review pointEvidence to keep
ReferenceControlled sample or standard, colour region and revision.
MethodNamed equation, relevant parameters and calculation settings.
MeasurementsInstrument/setup, backing, positions and applicable measurement condition.
DecisionAgreed limit, aggregation rule, exceptions and named colour approver.

Before approving the order

Hold a numerical pass/fail decision when reports use unidentified or different methods. Ask for a comparison on the agreed basis before changing ink or rejecting a batch. A non-critical internal label may only need an approved visual reference; do not add a measurement promise that neither party has agreed to deliver.
