Dark Tire Label Artwork Needs the Converter’s Total-Area-Coverage Limit

Published November 23, 2026

A tire-brand layout has a large dark panel assembled from several process inks. The composite PDF looks acceptable, but the converter asks for an ink-limit review before preparing the production file. Obtain the limit and the actual conversion context before changing colour recipes or treating the existing proof as a manufacturing approval.

Evaluate the same location in the actual output file

Total area coverage concerns the sum of channel percentages at a particular location. As fictional arithmetic only, C60 + M50 + Y50 + K100 totals 260 percent at that location. It is not the percentage of the entire label covered, a coating mass or a recommended limit. The calculation alone says nothing about whether the proposed production route accepts it.

Ask the converter to identify the relevant press or device, stock, ink set and output conversion, then supply the applicable coverage limit and its scope. Do not copy a value from another substrate or assume every extra white, varnish or technical separation belongs in the same calculation. Prepress should define the included channels and inspect the correct production revision.

Have prepress mark affected regions in its coverage preview and return any proposed correction under revision control. Reducing a total can change appearance; obtain the brand owner’s approval of the resulting proof on the intended construction. Keep fine black text, codes and their clear areas in separate checks so a dark-background correction does not silently change their approved treatment.

Esko’s press-setting documentation describes local summed coverage and a press/substrate-related maximum. Its viewer can help identify regions to review, but a preview is not evidence that the printed label has cured, bonded or met a service requirement. Retain the normal representative finished-output acceptance after the artwork question is resolved.

Esko DeskPack 16’s press-setting reference, Limited Total Area Coverage supports the prepress principle. It describes a named software version, not CYJ equipment or a universal ink limit.

What to include in the approval record

Review pointEvidence to keep
Output basisExact production artwork, conversion and relevant process/stock.
LimitConverter-supplied value and included-channel definition.
ReviewIdentified locations, coverage observation and controlled correction.
ReleaseAccepted colour proof plus independent code, fine-text and finished-print checks.

Before approving the order

Hold if nobody can identify which output and limit the warning concerns. If the artwork already meets the applicable condition, keep the approved recipe instead of reducing ink simply to produce a smaller number. A single-colour job may not need this multi-channel comparison.

For custom tire labels, send the released artwork, converter’s coverage requirement, marked dark areas, colour reference, stock/finish and quantities for a production-proof review.
