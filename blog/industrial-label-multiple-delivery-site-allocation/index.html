Industrial Label Deliveries to Several Sites: Approve the Allocation Matrix

Published November 4, 2026

A buyer approves 1,000 industrial labels, then asks for some to go to one plant and the balance to another. The total is clear. Which versions, roll groups and quantities belong at each receiving point may still be unresolved.

Before a proposed split delivery, approve a site-by-SKU allocation matrix and ask the supplier to confirm whether that packing and dispatch arrangement is feasible. State the artwork or data revision in each row. Destination allocation is an additional instruction: it should not silently change the information printed on an asset or equipment label.

Keep three identities separate

The label item identifies the product being supplied. A roll or pack reference identifies a physical group. A delivery-site reference identifies where that group is to be received. These references can be linked without being identical.

For example, a carton going to Plant A can contain equipment labels whose approved asset identities do not include Plant A in the printed text. Do not add a site prefix or restart a serial range merely to simplify packing. The data owner decides the valid printed values; the shipping instruction decides where the approved material goes.

Reconcile by SKU as well as by destination

Suppose a hypothetical order contains 600 of label item EQ-A, revision 2, and 400 of EQ-B, revision 3. Plant North needs 200 of each. Plant South needs 400 of EQ-A and 200 of EQ-B. The matrix totals are 400 for North, 600 for South, 600 for EQ-A and 400 for EQ-B. These figures are illustrative, not a shipment or a recommended pack size.

Check both directions. A total of 1,000 shipped labels is not sufficient evidence that each plant has the right mix. A changed destination requirement should identify the affected rows and preserve the overall purchase quantities unless the buyer separately authorizes a quantity change.

Approve packing before dispatch

  1. Assign unambiguous receiving-site references and confirm the full addresses and contacts through the agreed private order channel.
  2. List the SKU, revision, quantity, format and any controlled serial allocation for each site.
  3. Ask how rolls or packs will be identified and whether mixed-SKU cartons or partial rolls are proposed.
  4. Ask for a packing record that connects each physical pack to the relevant matrix rows.
  5. Confirm dispatch responsibilities, separate freight or packing effects, and the acknowledgement needed before a changed allocation is used.

CYJ's shipping information asks for destination and project details. It does not promise any particular split-delivery service or cost. Obtain confirmation for the actual order rather than treating this worksheet as an available logistics package.

Review special data allocations separately

For repeated equipment-information labels, quantity by item may be enough for the packing plan. For unique asset IDs, the buyer may need an explicit set or range assigned to each receiving team. First and last values alone may not reveal permitted gaps or copy counts.

Keep the approved data export under the data owner's control. The finished-roll sequence guide explains why file order does not automatically establish how labels are presented after finishing. Request an allocation the supplier can actually pack and the receiving team can reconcile.

Receive against the same matrix revision

Give each receiving team the rows it needs and the applicable revision of the allocation record. Check pack references, label item, artwork revision and quantity. If a destination receives unexpected material, preserve its identity and refer the discrepancy to the order owner before forwarding, applying or relabelling it.

Keep a later transfer between plants distinct from the original supplier delivery record. Otherwise a correct dispatch may appear short after stock moves internally. This is a proposed coordination method, not a claim that a particular supplier or warehouse follows it.

Send a multi-site label brief with approved items, quantities by site, roll format, data-allocation needs and requested delivery dates. Share private addresses and supporting allocation files through the existing email or WhatsApp route.
