Stationary Process Containers: Specify the Vessel-to-Placard Link Before Printing

Published November 14, 2026

A plant plans to replace markings on a fixed process vessel. Nearby pipes and another tank make a shared sign easy to misread. Before asking a printer to produce a wall placard or vessel marking, have the site safety owner confirm the association between each vessel and the approved workplace information.

Document where the information lives and who can reach it

For a US workplace under OSHA Hazard Communication, the stationary-process-container provision can allow specified alternatives to individually affixed labels when its conditions are met. Have the safety owner decide whether this provision applies. Use a marked-up photo or drawing to link the vessel, its installed reference and the exact sign, placard or other approved material. Confirm accessibility to employees in the area through each work shift.

Have the operating team witness the link at the real work position and record how a change of contents triggers a review and replacement. Specify the approved wording, surface or sign location, visibility and physical environment to the printer. A sign design cannot establish that a workplace program satisfies the rule. If the work involves a portable transfer bottle or a shipment, ask the safety owner for that use’s separate requirements.

The U.S. Government Publishing Office’s 2024 edition of 29 CFR 1910.1200 sets out the stationary process container option in paragraph (f)(7). The safety owner should review the current rule and the exact plant conditions.

Review pointEvidence to keep
AssociationVessel reference, precise position, approved hazard information and linked sign or record.
AccessActual work positions and how the approved information is available during each shift.
Change controlOwner, change trigger and replacement or withdrawal of outdated material.
Print handoffApproved artwork, mounted size, surface and actual exposure.

Before approving the order

Stop the print release if the sign could apply to more than one vessel without a clear link, or if the safety owner has not approved the information arrangement. Use an actual location witness before ordering copies for additional vessels.

For chemical labels, send the safety-approved artwork, vessel map, sign dimensions and actual exposure conditions.
