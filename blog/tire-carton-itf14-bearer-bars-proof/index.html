ITF-14 on Tire Carton Labels: Review the Bearer Bars for the Print Route

Published November 12, 2026

A buyer moves the tire case identifier from direct printing on corrugated board to a printed adhesive label. The receiving partner already requires ITF-14. Before reusing the carton artwork, confirm the required bearer treatment for the label production method.

Identify what the converter will actually print

Ask whether the proof represents direct printing on the carton or printing on separate label stock. Mark the bearer bars, code area, clear sides and readable digits. Check that a decorative border has not been mistaken for the approved bearer treatment or placed inside the required clear area.

Have prepress use the approved identifier and confirm the symbol specification for the print process. Maintain the complete final dimensions on the proof. If artwork is cropped to fit the label, inspect the actual output and its clear space rather than relying on the old carton drawing. Keep the substrate and final viewing condition with the approved sample.

GS1 US’s ITF-14 guidance describes a full bearer surround for direct corrugated printing and notes that the vertical sides are unnecessary on a label. Use the applicable GS1 and receiving specifications for the exact geometry and acceptance conditions.

Review pointEvidence to record before ordering
Print routeDirect carton or separate label, with actual material and printing method.
ArtworkApproved GTIN, bearer treatment, complete quiet zones, readable digits and finished dimensions.
AcceptanceApplied finished sample, receiving scan and any specified verification report.

When to release the order

Hold the artwork when the print route changes and no approved proof exists for the new construction. If the receiving specification asks for additional encoded fields, confirm the carrier before working on bearer geometry. The buyer’s data owner approves the value and the converter confirms reproduction on the agreed print route.
