Printed Data Matrix Labels and Direct Part Marks: Do Not Swap Verification Methods

Published November 6, 2026

A parts buyer requests an adhesive Data Matrix label but copies a verification requirement from a drawing for a directly marked metal component. The supplier offers a grade, and both sides assume the number means the same thing. The physical marking process and the report method have not been reconciled.

Identify the supplied marking before agreeing acceptance. A printed label attached to a part and a symbol formed directly on the part can require different quality-evaluation arrangements. The fact that both encode Data Matrix does not resolve which application specification governs them.

Name the marking process in the purchase record

ISO's description of ISO/IEC 29158:2025 identifies a direct-part-mark quality specification that modifies the methodology in ISO/IEC 15415, including illumination and reporting. The published scope is a reason to distinguish the methods, not a rule that procurement should choose settings without the application owner.

Write whether the order is for a printed adhesive label, a directly marked component, or two separately specified identification routes. If a drawing names one process while the purchase order requests another, return the conflict for a documented decision. Do not quietly relabel a report to make the two documents appear consistent.

Separate the acceptance questions

QuestionResponsible inputEvidence to retain
What is supplied?Engineering drawing and agreed order scopeMarking process and construction identity
Which quality method applies?Customer application specification and technical reviewerStandard, edition, required criteria and reporting basis
Does the operational reader work?Actual reader and installed workflowSample identity and observed result
Is the encoded identity correct?Approved source dataDecoded-to-master reconciliation

Agreement on a quality method does not establish that the correct serial number was printed. A successful operational scan does not erase a contractual verification requirement. Our scan-versus-verification guide explains those separate checks; here the additional issue is identifying the appropriate report basis for the marking being purchased.

Compare reports only after their basis is clear

Request the method and edition, symbol identity, measurement configuration, sample or lot reference and any applicable customer criteria. Have the qualified reviewer assess the details required by the governing specification. Two reports showing the same headline grade are not automatically interchangeable when their methods or conditions differ.

If the offered report concerns a different marking process, record the gap. The next step may be a corrected requirement, relevant evidence or an authorized engineering change. It is not a mathematical conversion invented from two grade numbers. This website does not claim a particular verifier, measurement service or approved verification capability.

Check the label as a label too

When an adhesive label is the agreed supply, its surface, placement, attachment and required readable lifetime still need evaluation. A print-quality report cannot qualify adhesion to an oily component or prove that the label survives the buyer's cleaning process. Conversely, a durable attachment does not establish symbol quality.

Where the required identity must remain after the proposed label is removed or replaced, engineering should decide whether an adhesive route is appropriate at all. Keep any change from a permanent direct mark to a replaceable label explicit; the printer should not make that lifecycle decision by adjusting artwork.

For industrial barcode labels, send a marking-and-acceptance brief with the relevant drawing excerpt, proposed supply type, code and data rules, exact customer verification requirement, part photo, surface and quantities. Ask the application owner to resolve conflicting requirements before production approval. Source checked November 6, 2026.
