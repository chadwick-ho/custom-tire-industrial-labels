Clear Equipment Labels: Approve the White Underprint Edge With the Colour Artwork

Published November 25, 2026

A buyer approves a clear equipment-label design with white backing behind coloured information. The composite preview looks opaque, but it does not establish how the backing edge meets the colour in production. After confirming the supplier’s actual construction, approve the edge geometry and representative finished output.

Review the backing alone and in the intended alignment

First confirm whether the proposed label really uses printed white backing or a different opaque construction. This guide concerns a confirmed white-underprint route. It does not prescribe white ink for every clear label or establish that a supplier offers the process.

Ask prepress to provide the backing separation and an aligned overlay against the approved colour objects. Identify regions that must remain transparent and regions that need backing for readable information. Include small text, fine details, corners and any code background as separate review points.

A backing made smaller than the supported colour outline is commonly described as choked or inset; one made larger is spread or outset. Have the converter define its actual operation and supported offset for this construction. An arbitrary universal offset cannot be selected from a screen picture.

If backing and colour do not align as intended, exposed backing or unsupported edge regions may change the appearance. Reducing backing too far can also remove support from a narrow feature. Have the converter show the resulting geometry at finished size and explain what the actual process can maintain. The exaggerated illustrations show the decision, not a measured defect or supplier tolerance.

Review a representative finished sample on the intended equipment surface under the agreed viewing conditions. Check the smallest backed feature and the perimeter as well as the large colour area. A code’s required light background and quiet area need their own qualified review; do not shrink them casually as part of a decorative edge adjustment.

Esko ArtPro+ 25.11 documentation, White Underprint section describes creating larger or smaller underprint objects with spread/choke controls. The converter must confirm the actual production workflow, capabilities and geometry; a software control does not establish print registration performance.

What to include in the approval record

Review pointEvidence to keep
ConstructionConfirmed white-underprint route, face, ink/finish proposal and actual substrate.
GeometryIsolated backing, aligned overlay, transparent exclusions and converter-approved edge treatment.
Small featuresFinished-size narrow details, corners and separately controlled code-background areas.
Physical releaseIdentifiable sample, actual surface, observed edges and owner-approved result.

Before approving the order

Hold the edge treatment when the backing channel or production capability is unresolved. Ask the converter whether a simpler opaque label, an altered clear region or another supported layout better serves the job.

A larger or smaller backing is not automatically better. Keep the approved geometry with the artwork revision and recheck when a changed size or detail no longer preserves the same features.

For equipment machinery labels, send the colour artwork and backing map, actual surface colour, smallest critical features, intended construction and quantities.
