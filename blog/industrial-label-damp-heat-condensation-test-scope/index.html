Damp-Heat Label Reports Need the Condensation Condition

Published November 23, 2026

An equipment OEM asks whether a label will remain usable when a cabinet experiences humid temperature changes. A supplier sends a report headed damp heat. Before accepting it, determine whether the report concerns noncondensing humidity or a cycle intended to produce surface condensation. Similar report headings do not make the exposure histories equivalent.

Read the test scope before comparing hours

Ask the equipment owner to describe conditions at the actual label position, including temperature changes, moisture sources and when information must be read. Keep the physical service description separate from the chosen qualification method. The label buyer should not infer the appropriate chamber cycle, severity or acceptance limit from a general humidity percentage.

The public scope of IEC 60068-2-78:2025 describes high humidity at constant temperature without condensation. IEC 60068-2-30:2025 describes cyclic temperature changes at high humidity, generally producing condensation on the specimen. These are method-scope distinctions. Neither public abstract supplies a universal label qualification or service-life conversion.

Have the responsible technical reviewer select the applicable method and revision, conditions and duration. Then identify the actual tested assembly: mounting substrate and finish, label face, adhesive, printing, protection, dimensions and application history. A blank film suspended in a chamber cannot silently stand for a printed adhesive label on a coated panel.

Agree when the witness will be inspected and read. An observation during a wet phase, one after recovery and one after drying can answer different questions. Record attachment, print condition, visual appearance and required code results separately. If only the recovered specimen was examined, retain that limit instead of describing its information as readable throughout exposure.

IEC’s public description of IEC 60068-2-78:2025 supports the noncondensing steady-state scope. The linked cyclic-method description supplies the comparison; the complete applicable procedure belongs with the qualified test owner.

What to include in the approval record

Review pointEvidence to keep
Service questionActual cabinet location, temperature/moisture history and reading point.
MethodNamed standard and revision, agreed conditions and exposure sequence.
AssemblyIdentified finished label on the representative mounting surface.
ObservationDuring-exposure and recovery checks, criteria and responsible reviewer.

Before approving the order

Hold if the application needs condensation evidence but the report only establishes a different humidity condition. Keep an appropriate existing report where it matches the agreed task. The quotation should identify what evidence is available, not promise a chamber test or lifetime that the supplier has not confirmed.

For special environment labels, send the actual moisture and temperature sequence, mounting surface, supplier report, required reading stage, label artwork and quantities for an exposure review.
