A Tire RFID Label Brief Needs the Actual Receiving Regions

Published November 22, 2026

A distributor wants the same tire identification arrangement at the packing site and two overseas receiving warehouses. A proposal says Gen2 compatible. Before treating that phrase as worldwide approval, have the RFID owner identify every intended read location and confirm the radio requirements and proposed hardware for each. The printed tire-label brief still needs its own approved data and fallback task.

Ask for site coverage rather than a universal range claim

List the encoding and receiving locations, whether a read is required at each, and the responsible system owner. Ask that owner to supply the current locally applicable operating requirements and the actual reader configuration. Keep the offered tag or inlay reference, antenna and relevant technical data linked to those requirements. A broad technology name cannot replace that comparison.

The GS1 Gen2 standard defines an air-interface protocol over a stated frequency scope. Its regional discussion leaves operating requirements to local rules. This article does not assign permitted frequencies or power levels to a country, nor establish equipment authorization. The qualified radio-system owner must confirm those details for the actual sites and date.

Request representative evidence using the proposed identification construction on the intended tire or package, under the receiving arrangement that will be used. Agree which locations and configurations the evidence covers. A successful trial at the packing plant can remain useful without qualifying every destination. If destination arrangements are still unknown, keep the radio portion pending while resolving the optical label’s independent requirements.

Confirm how staff recover the intended record from the printed barcode or readable value if the radio path is unavailable. Use the same owner-approved identity mapping, but evaluate optical access at its own scan point. This is a purchasing coordination guide; it does not claim CYJ supplies embedded tire tags, readers or radio integration.

GS1’s Gen2 air-interface standard, release 3.0.1 provides protocol scope and distinguishes it from local operating requirements. Use current site-specific review for implementation.

What to include in the approval record

Review pointEvidence to keep
LocationsActual encoding and read sites, required events and responsible owners.
OfferNamed tag/inlay and reader arrangement with technical data references.
ScopeSite operating requirements, configuration and evidence covered by approval.
FallbackPrinted payload, physical placement and exception-handling witness.

Before approving the order

Hold a cross-region radio approval if destination sites are absent from the brief or a protocol claim is its only evidence. Where the task only requires printed labels, keep the purchase focused on optical identification rather than adding an unsupported RFID requirement.

Related reading includes the optical and radio carrier comparison, the receiving-event trial, the printed fallback mapping.

For tire barcode labels, send the approved printed fallback fields, intended read locations, tire photos, label dimensions and quantities for the optical label brief.
